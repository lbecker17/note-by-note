# Singing-lesson and pitch-training apps: the 2026 landscape and what users say about them

## Which apps lead, and what are their teaching approach, features, pricing and platforms?

### Takeaway
Leading apps split into a few types:
- Structured courses: Yousician, Singing Carrots, SingSharp, Erol Singer's Studio.
- Song-first karaoke-with-scoring: Simply Sing, Smule, Singa, KaraFun.
- Exercise and scale tools: Swiftscales, Vanido, VoCo.
- Plain pitch monitors and web ear-trainers: Pitchy Ninja, CheckYourPitch, Theta Music Trainer.
- A 2025–26 wave of "AI vocal coach" apps: Singing Coach AI, PitchFlow, EchoVQ, Vocal Tuner AI.

Nearly all of the structured options are paid subscriptions of roughly US$55–120 a year. The only free option often cited is Vanido, and it has no curriculum.

## What do user reviews praise and complain about?

### Takeaway
Users most praise automatic key-fitting ("the key slider") and quick early pitch gains. Common complaints:
- inaccurate or inconsistent pitch and range detection, especially in noisy rooms;
- voice-type labels users disagree with;
- paywalls that block trying songs;
- glitches after paying;
- streaks that feel disconnected from real improvement.

Reddit could not be searched, so Reddit community sentiment is a gap.

### Cited Findings
**Pitch and range detection accuracy**
- [USR] Simply Sing's range test gives different results each time the same user repeats it. — [search summary of App Store / JustUseApp reviews](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1592900217/simply-sing-by-joytunes/reviews)

Complaints have persisted across several update cycles, and community members often recommend an external USB microphone. — [Top Consumer Reviews](https://www.topconsumerreviews.com/best-singing-lessons/reviews/yousician.php)

- [USR] Users complain of paying and then hitting glitches. Price is judged "not worth it", and the app "doesn't even give a couple of free songs to try" before asking for payment. — [JustUseApp reviews](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1592900217/simply-sing-by-joytunes/reviews)

**What users praise**
- [USR] Users praise Simply Sing's key adjustment. One reviewer says it helped them "discover their correct voice type". Several say their pitch improved noticeably, one "throughout one hour" of use. — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/simply-sing-learn-to-sing/id1592900217?l=es) (per search summary)
- [JRN] Yousician's overall ratings are Trustpilot 4.8/5, Google Play 4.4/5 and App Store 4.6/5. Headline ratings are high even though the complaints are specific. — [Top Consumer Reviews](https://www.topconsumerreviews.com/best-singing-lessons/reviews/yousician.php)

**Motivation and gamification**
- [USR/developer anecdote] A small team building a "Duolingo for singing" added streaks, XP, leagues and a mascot. The metrics improved for only about three weeks. In interviews, users said "I keep my streak going but I don't think I'm actually getting better." The team concluded that feedback measuring only one dimension (pitch) optimises users toward that dimension alone, which they call Goodhart's law. — [DEV Community, "We Tried to Build 'Duolingo for Singing'…"](https://dev.to/max_polaczuk/we-tried-to-build-duolingo-for-singing-with-a-tiny-team-heres-what-actually-worked-4k61)

### Inferences
- Detection reliability (noise, range-test inconsistency) is the technical complaint most likely to destroy trust. A range test that gives different answers each run undermines everything that is transposed from it.
- Confident voice-type labels (soprano, alto, contralto, and so on) create disputes. Reporting range as note names avoids them.
- Paywall frustration is common across the category, so being fully free is a real differentiator.

## Which features are distinctive or new in 2025–26, and is there evidence they work?

### Takeaway
The 2025–26 novelty is "AI vocal coaching": LLM chat plus analysis beyond pitch (breathiness, formants and vowels, onsets, vibrato rate and extent, "tension"), song-track splitting, and breath detection. The only outcome data is vendor-published and not peer-reviewed.

Peer-reviewed evidence exists only for the older core feature, real-time visual pitch feedback. That evidence is positive but nuanced:
- Accuracy often drops while watching the display and then improves afterwards.
- Beginners do better with detailed pitch-contour displays.
- At least one 10-week RCT found no advantage for concurrent feedback over practice without it once the feedback was removed, which points to a risk of dependence on the display.

### Cited Findings
**New features and marketing claims (2025–26)**
- [MKT] Singing Carrots claims to be "the only [AI coach] with published outcome data across 2,000+ singers and 13,000+ coaching sessions". It says beginners gained +16.5 percentage points in pitch accuracy in four weeks. This is not peer-reviewed; the comparison group and how accuracy was measured were not visible. — [Singing Carrots blog (vendor)](https://singingcarrots.com/blog/top-7-ai-vocal-coaches/)
- [MKT] Newer apps claim "multimodal analysis" that goes beyond pitch tracking:
  - spectral features for tone quality and breathiness;
  - formants for vowel placement;
  - onset characteristics;
  - vibrato rate and extent.
  — [Bloom Vocal on Singing Coach AI (vendor)](https://www.bloomvocal.site/en/blog/singing-coach-ai-review-2026)
- [MKT] Singing Coach AI (Dec 2025) analyses uploaded recordings and adds an LLM-style conversational coach. — [Bloom Vocal (vendor)](https://www.bloomvocal.site/en/blog/singing-coach-ai-review-2026)
- [MKT] PitchFlow claims AI analysis of "vocal tension". — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.pitchflow.vocal)
- [MKT] SingSharp offers breath detection plus analysis of tone and resonance. — [Singing Carrots blog (vendor)](https://singingcarrots.com/blog/top-7-ai-vocal-coaches/)

**Peer-reviewed evidence on visual pitch feedback**
- [PR] **Wilson, Lee, Callaghan & Thorpe (2008), *Journal of Interdisciplinary Music Studies*** ("Learning to sing in tune: does real-time visual feedback help?"):
  - Accuracy fell below baseline during training with real-time visual feedback, then rose significantly above baseline at post-test. The authors attribute the dip to cognitive load from the display.
  - Beginners were more accurate with a detailed pitch-contour display; advanced students did better with a simple categorical (right/wrong) display.
  - Sample size not retrieved.
  — [search summary of the review literature](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/12/21/10781); [ResearchGate listing](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/251823345_Learning_to_sing_in_tune_Does_real-time_visual_feedback_help)
- [PR] **Berglin, Pfordresher & Demorest (2022), *Psychology of Music*** — adult poor-pitch singers:
  - Three groups: vocal pitch-matching training with visual feedback in **Erol Singer's Studio on an iPad**; the same exercises as audio only (singing along to MIDI piano mp3s); and a control group.
  - All groups improved somewhat from pre- to post-test, but training was significant **only in the visual-feedback group**. The effect was strongest on 4-note melodies rather than single pitches.
  - The authors propose that visual feedback substitutes for inaccurate auditory–motor links.
  - Sample size not retrieved.
  — [Berglin et al. 2022](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/03057356211026730); [author preprint](https://ubwp.buffalo.edu/apal-pfordresher/wp-content/uploads/sites/79/2022/01/Berglin_Pfordresher_Demorest_PoM_inpress.pdf)
- [PR] **Paney & Tharp, *Psychology of Music*** (ResearchGate record from 2019; the search summary says 2021):
  - Adult non-music-major university students were randomly assigned for 10 weeks to a game giving concurrent visual feedback on accuracy (SingingCoach) or a control condition.
  - **Both groups improved significantly; the feedback group was not significantly better.** The feedback group scored better during training, but after feedback was removed its retention scores converged with the control group's, which suggests "guidance dependency".
  - Participants' ratings of their own singing rose.
  - Sample size not retrieved.
  — [ResearchGate record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/334113826_The_effect_of_concurrent_visual_feedback_on_adult_singing_accuracy) (per search summary)
- [PR] **Paney (2015), *Music Education Research* 17(1)**:
  - 33 undergraduate non-musicians played a scored singing video game (the Rock Band / Karaoke Revolution type) for **10 minutes**.
  - Pitch-matching scores differed significantly between pre- and post-test.
  - This shows an immediate effect only; retention was not measured.
  — [ERIC EJ1049415](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1049415); [T&F](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/14613808.2014.969218)
- [PR, attribution uncertain] One search summary stated two further points. I could not confirm which of the listed papers they came from; possibly [Frontiers 2021, violin and voice pitch matching](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.684693/full). Treat them as unverified:
  - Trained singers benefited more from visual feedback than untrained ones.
  - Skilled singers' accuracy dropped under visual feedback because of the extra cognitive load.

**Voice-type detection**
- [USR] The only evidence found is user dispute of Simply Sing's classifications (see the user-reviews section). No validation study of app voice-type detection was found.

### Inferences
- Note by Note's scrolling pitch lane is a detailed contour display. Wilson et al. (2008) suggest that is the right choice for beginners.
- Two studies caution that performance *while watching* the display is a poor guide to learning:
  - Wilson et al. saw a dip during training and gains afterwards.
  - Paney & Tharp saw feedback-group gains vanish once the feedback was removed.
- These results match the general motor-learning principle that feedback given constantly can create dependence. That principle was not sourced in this session, so it counts as an inference here.
- The peer-reviewed positive result with an iPad app (Berglin 2022) is the closest evidence that a phone-style pitch-feedback app can help adult poor-pitch singers.
- No evidence was found that LLM coaching, timbre scores, "tension" detection or automatic voice-type classification improve outcomes. These are marketing claims.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed evaluation of any 2025–26 "AI vocal coach" or LLM feedback system was found.
- There is no independent validation of Singing Carrots' +16.5 pp claim: its method, control group, drop-outs and definition of "accuracy" are unknown.
- Nothing found on:
  - evidence for vibrato meters, duet or harmony practice, or video posture feedback in apps;
  - evidence on whether recording and self-comparison helps self-taught adults.
- The 2026 arXiv survey "30+ Years of Automatic Singing Assessment" ([arXiv 2601.12153](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.12153v1)) likely covers these topics but was not fetchable.
- The Pfordresher & Greenspon (2025) "45% → 72%" figure (see the next section) is unverified.

## What do voice teachers and research say about learning singing from apps, and what are the common pitfalls?

### Takeaway
Across teacher blogs, the consensus is that apps are good for daily practice, pitch matching and consistency. They cannot see or feel technique problems (strain, jaw and neck tension, posture, breath), so repeating app exercises while straining can cause harm.

Research adds three points that matter for a self-guided app:
- Most poor pitch comes from mapping heard pitch onto the voice, not from poor hearing.
- Poor-pitch singers can be *hindered* by singing along with a correct guide.
- Training across a wider pitch range (an octave) remediates better than a narrow one.

### Cited Findings
**Research on poor-pitch singing**
- [PR] **Pfordresher & Brown (2007), *Music Perception* 25:95–115**:
  - Across two experiments, about 10–15% of participants were "poor-pitch singers", defined as imitating pitch at least 1 semitone off.
  - They did **not** differ from good singers in pitch discrimination. The problem was mapping pitch onto action, not hearing.
  - Poor-pitch singers "appeared to be hindered rather than helped by singing with correct accompaniment".
  — [author PDF](http://www.acsu.buffalo.edu/~pqp/pdfs/Pfordresher&Brown_2007_MP.pdf); [UC Press](http://mp.ucpress.edu/content/25/2/95)
- [PR] **Pfordresher et al. (2010), *JASA*, "Imprecise singing is widespread"**:
  - Accuracy (mean deviation from target) differs from precision (consistency across repeated attempts).
  - Inaccurate singers were, with one exception, also imprecise. Accurate singers were equally likely to be precise or imprecise.
  - Poor singing is more prevalent when judged by precision than by accuracy.
  — [PubMed 20968388](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20968388/); [author PDF](http://www.acsu.buffalo.edu/~pqp/pdfs/Pfordresher_etal_2010_JASA.pdf)
- [PR] **Pfordresher & Greenspon (2025), *Musicae Scientiae* 29:240–255**:
  - A restricted vocal range during pitch matching is a symptom of poor singing "not reducible to physical vocal limitations".
  - Training that matched pitches across **one octave** was compared with training across **a perfect fifth (7 semitones)**. Only the wide-range group improved significantly, and only for single-pitch matching, not 4-note melodies.
  - The authors call this partial support for wide-range training.
  - The search summary gives single-pitch accuracy rising "from 45% to 72%" in the wide-range group; unverified.
  — [Musicae Scientiae](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/10298649241289542) (per search summary)
- [PR] **Pfordresher & Demorest (2021), *JRME***: a population-scale online study (N = 632) using the Seattle Singing Accuracy Protocol. Prevalence percentages were not retrieved. — [JRME](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0022429420951630); [author PDF](https://ubwp.buffalo.edu/apal-pfordresher/wp-content/uploads/sites/79/2022/01/PfordresherDemorest_2021_JRME.pdf)

**Teacher and practitioner views**
- [PED/MKT] "Most apps labeled 'AI vocal coach' are really pitch detection with gamification." They can tell you whether you hit the note, but not why you are struggling or whether technique is causing strain. — [Singing Carrots blog (vendor; self-interested)](https://singingcarrots.com/blog/top-7-ai-vocal-coaches/)
- [PED] If you strain on high notes, repeating app exercises blindly "will not help you at all, it will actually cause harm". A generic program "can't feel the discomfort you feel", whereas a teacher spots strain early. — [Kunstplaza, app vs teacher comparison](https://www.kunstplaza.de/en/music/learn-to-sing-app-singing-teachers-comparison/)
- [PED] An app may detect being off-pitch but "can't adjust your stance, relax your jaw, or ease your nerves". — [search summary of teacher blogs](https://www.encoreacademyut.com/blogs/voice-lessons-app); see also [Rayvox](https://blog.rayvox.co.uk/why-singing-teachers-still-matter-in-an-ai-dominated-world)
- [PED] The recommended pattern: use apps for daily warmups, pitch training and consistency, and a teacher for technique. — [Preply](https://preply.com/en/blog/best-singing-learning-apps/); [singwell.eu, "tested by a voice teacher"](https://singwell.eu/singing-apps/)
- [PED] Singers make "great progress with their ear-training and pitch matching" when they use apps consistently. — [Alexis Hightower](https://www.alexishightower.com/topics/my-favorite-apps-for-singers)
- [Developer opinion, not empirical] Pitch is "only about 20%" of good singing; tone, breath, vowels, tension and dynamics make up the rest. — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/max_polaczuk/we-tried-to-build-duolingo-for-singing-with-a-tiny-team-heres-what-actually-worked-4k61)

### Inferences
- The research suggests that "tone deaf" self-labels are usually wrong for adult beginners, which is encouraging. It also implies a product-design message: "your ears are probably fine; this is a coordination skill".
- Pfordresher & Brown's accompaniment finding bears directly on Note by Note's headphone mode, where the guide note plays while the user sings. For a subset of learners, that mode may mask or hinder learning. Call-and-response, which the no-headphones mode already uses, may be the better *assessment* condition.
- Two findings pull in opposite directions:
  - Wide-range training helps (Pfordresher & Greenspon 2025).
  - Pushing the range carries a strain risk (teacher consensus).
  A range-fitted octave that is expanded gradually, with explicit comfort and strain checks, reconciles them.

### Gaps
- I couldn't retrieve canonical pedagogy sources (for example Richard Miller, *The Structure of Singing*; Welch's work on pitch development; NATS guidance on technology). The "pedagogical consensus" above comes from teacher blogs, not texts.
- No sourced evidence was found on vocal-fatigue or practice-duration guidance for adult self-learners; other researchers may cover this.
- Sample sizes for Berglin et al. (2022), Paney & Tharp and Wilson et al. (2008) were not retrieved.

## Where are the gaps a small, free, private PWA could fill?

### Takeaway
The market is crowded with paid, account-based, often cloud-analysed apps whose weak points are detection trust, paywalls and shallow gamification. A free, no-account, on-device PWA can compete on:
- price and privacy;
- transparency about what is measured;
- evidence-informed practice design that competitors don't advertise, such as faded feedback, measuring consistency and unaccompanied checks.

It cannot credibly compete on song catalogue size or on "AI technique analysis".

- [USR] Streaks feel hollow without visible improvement. — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/max_polaczuk/we-tried-to-build-duolingo-for-singing-with-a-tiny-team-heres-what-actually-worked-4k61)

### Inferences
- An Australian adult beginner's options are mostly paid in USD or EUR. Being free, offline-capable and account-free is a genuine niche, and so is the local repertoire (Waltzing Matilda).
- Privacy is under-marketed in this category. "Nothing is recorded or uploaded" could be a headline feature, though this depends on competitors' actual data practices, which were not checked.
- Beyond privacy, a small app can differentiate by being *honest*: saying what the detector can and can't judge, and not claiming technique or timbre analysis it can't do.

### Gaps
- Competitors' privacy policies and data practices (whether audio is uploaded, whether accounts are required) were not checked.
- iOS Safari PWA constraints (microphone permissions, AudioContext latency, Bluetooth headphone latency, background behaviour) were not researched here.
- I found no data on how many adult beginners use apps as their *only* tool, or on drop-out rates.

## Implications for a self-guided singing app

### Takeaway
Note by Note already lines up with the better-evidenced parts of the landscape:
- range-fitted transposition;
- a detailed pitch-contour display for beginners;
- short daily practice;
- a mix of single-note and melodic echo tasks.

The biggest evidence-backed improvements would be:
- **fade the visual feedback** so learners can show retention without it;
- **score unaccompanied (call-and-response) attempts** as the true test, because a guide tone can hinder poor-pitch singers;
- **report consistency, not just average error**;
- **extend pitch-matching across a comfortable octave**, with strain safeguards;
- **make the range test and detector more trustworthy** than competitors'.

### Cited Findings (the evidence each implication rests on)
- Accuracy dips *during* visual feedback and rises afterwards; beginners do better with detailed contour displays. — [PR] Wilson, Lee, Callaghan & Thorpe 2008, via [MDPI review](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/12/21/10781)
- Gains from concurrent feedback converged with the control group once feedback was removed ("guidance dependency"). — [PR] Paney & Tharp, [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/334113826_The_effect_of_concurrent_visual_feedback_on_adult_singing_accuracy)
- Visual-feedback training in an iPad app (Erol) beat audio-only practice for adult poor-pitch singers, especially on 4-note melodies. — [PR] [Berglin et al. 2022](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/03057356211026730)
- Poor-pitch singers are hindered by correct accompaniment; their pitch discrimination is normal. — [PR] [Pfordresher & Brown 2007](http://www.acsu.buffalo.edu/~pqp/pdfs/Pfordresher&Brown_2007_MP.pdf)
- Precision (consistency) is a separate, more widespread problem than accuracy. — [PR] [Pfordresher et al. 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20968388/)
- Octave-wide pitch-matching training beat fifth-wide training (single pitches). — [PR] [Pfordresher & Greenspon 2025](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/10298649241289542)
- Ten minutes of a scored singing game produced an immediate pitch-matching gain. — [PR] [Paney 2015](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1049415)
- Range tests that vary between runs, and disputed voice-type labels, frustrate users. — [USR] [JustUseApp](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1592900217/simply-sing-by-joytunes/reviews)
- Pitch detection fails in noisy rooms, and users are told to buy a mic. — [JRN] [Top Consumer Reviews](https://www.topconsumerreviews.com/best-singing-lessons/reviews/yousician.php)
- Apps can't detect strain; repeating exercises while straining causes harm. — [PED] [Kunstplaza](https://www.kunstplaza.de/en/music/learn-to-sing-app-singing-teachers-comparison/)
- Streaks without perceived improvement feel hollow; pitch-only feedback optimises only pitch. — [USR/dev] [DEV Community](https://dev.to/max_polaczuk/we-tried-to-build-duolingo-for-singing-with-a-tiny-team-heres-what-actually-worked-4k61)
- Paywalls before trying songs are a top complaint, and free alternatives lack a curriculum. — [USR] [JustUseApp](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1592900217/simply-sing-by-joytunes/reviews); [JRN] [American Songwriter](https://americansongwriter.com/best-singing-apps/)

### Inferences (concrete design implications)

**Faded feedback and memory checks**
1. **Add "eyes-off" or "blind" attempts and fade the lane.** After a learner succeeds with the pitch lane, have them sing the same line with the live pitch trace hidden (or the targets hidden) and reveal the trace only afterwards.
   - Make the blind score the one that unlocks progress.
   - Rests on: Paney & Tharp's guidance dependency; Wilson 2008's during-vs-after pattern.

**Scoring and assessment**
2. **Treat call-and-response (hear, then sing unaccompanied) as the scored condition, even with headphones.** Keep sing-along-with-guide as a warm-up or "training wheels" mode, and score it separately or label it "with help".
   - Rests on: Pfordresher & Brown 2007, where correct accompaniment hindered poor-pitch singers.
3. **Add a consistency metric next to average miss.** For example, the spread of cents error across notes or repeats ("your notes landed within ±X cents"), and a "repeat the same note 5 times" drill.
   - Rests on: Pfordresher et al. 2010 on accuracy vs precision.
   - The current flat/sharp tendency already measures accuracy bias. Consistency is the missing half.

**Range**
4. **Extend pitch-matching drills across a full comfortable octave, with random target order, not only stepwise five-note patterns.** Grow the span gradually as the range test permits.
   - Rests on: Pfordresher & Greenspon 2025. The evidence is partial: it improved single pitches but not melodies, and one study only.
5. **Pair range expansion with explicit strain safeguards.**
   - A pre-set "stop if it hurts, tickles or feels tight; hoarseness means rest" message.
   - A one-tap "that felt strained" button that trims the top and bottom notes from future exercises.
   - Short sessions.
   - Rests on: teacher consensus that apps can't detect strain; this is [PED], not empirical.
6. **Make the range test trustworthy.**
   - Take two or three attempts per end and use a robust value, such as the median or the second-best rather than the extreme.
   - Show the result as note names, with an option to adjust by hand.
   - Offer a re-test prompt every few weeks.
   - Avoid assigning voice-type labels, or present them as tentative.
   - Rests on: user complaints about Simply Sing's range test varying between runs and disputed voice-type labels.

**Detector trust**
7. **Make detector trust visible.**
   - A mic-level and "I can't hear you clearly" state, and a confidence threshold so noise isn't scored as a note.
   - Explicit handling of octave jumps, for example scoring pitch class with an octave-tolerance flag in early lessons and showing "you sang this an octave lower, which is fine" instead of a miss.
   - A one-line quiet-room and headphones tip.
   - Rests on: Yousician noise complaints [JRN]. Octave-error evidence is a gap, so this part is an inference.

**Display**
8. **Keep the detailed contour display for beginners, and offer a simpler hit/miss view later.**
   - Rests on: Wilson 2008, where beginners did better with contour and advanced singers with categorical feedback.

**Progress and motivation**
9. **Make the streak point at measurable progress.** Show weekly trends in average miss, consistency, blind-attempt score and range, so the streak reflects improvement rather than attendance.
   - Rests on: the DEV Community anecdote ([USR/dev], weak evidence).
10. **Keep sessions short and game-like.** The ~3-minute warmup is consistent with Paney 2015's 10-minute immediate gains, but retention evidence for such short sessions is lacking.
11. **Keep both single-note and short-melody tasks.** The echo game and song lines are the right mix: Berglin 2022's gains were largest on 4-note melodies, and Pfordresher & Greenspon 2025's on single pitches.

**Honesty about limits and the AI coach**
12. **Be honest about scope in the UI and in "Copy results for Claude".** The exported summary should state that the app measures only pitch and timing, so an LLM coach doesn't invent feedback on tone, breath or tension that it has no data for.
    - Include the data an LLM can use: accuracy, consistency, flat/sharp tendency by register, blind vs guided scores, and the strained-note flags.
    - Rests on: the "pitch is one dimension" critique [PED/dev]. There is no evidence of LLM coaching efficacy.

**Positioning**
13. **Lead with free, private and no-account.** Every lesson and song is available without a paywall, and nothing leaves the phone.
    - Rests on: paywall complaints [USR] and upload-based AI competitors [MKT]. Competitors' privacy practices were not verified.
14. **Optional future feature: on-device record-and-listen-back** (kept in memory, never uploaded) for self-listening to tone. Evidence for this was not found in this session, so it is flagged as a hypothesis, not a finding.

### Gaps
- Several implications rest on single studies with sample sizes I couldn't retrieve (Paney & Tharp; Wilson 2008; Berglin 2022) or on unverified figures (Pfordresher & Greenspon's 45% → 72%). Before these become firm design rules, the report writer should present them as "promising, limited evidence".
- I found no empirical evidence on:
  - safe range-expansion rates for untrained adults;
  - headphone vs speaker practice outcomes;
  - whether blind-attempt gating hurts motivation.
