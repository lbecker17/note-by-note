# Foundational singing technique for adult beginners: curriculum, sequencing, exercises, faults, and what an app can measure

Where I add content from background knowledge of a well-known textbook, I mark it "(background knowledge, not re-verified this session)". ## 1. What do the major traditions agree a beginner curriculum covers in the first 3–12 months, and in what order?

### Takeaway
Classical (Miller, McKinney, Titze) and CCM (LoVetri, Estill, SLS, CVT) pedagogy agree on the core building blocks: alignment, breath management, a coordinated (balanced) onset, efficient phonation (neither breathy nor pressed), vowel/resonance work, and joining the registers. They disagree mainly on how explicit the anatomy should be and on the aesthetic goals. Onset plus breath coordination comes first in Miller's sequence. Registration (chest/head/"mix") is the main CCM priority. The empirical evidence under specific breathing directives is weaker than the tradition implies.

### Cited Findings
**Classical tradition [P]**
- Richard Miller's *The Structure of Singing* covers technique in chapters on vocal onset and release, breath management, resonance and registration, with exercises for each. It aims to refine the coordination of breathing, phonation and resonance. — [Book synopsis via search](https://www.csmusic.net/content/book-synopsis-of-richard-millers-the-structure-of-singing/); [Internet Archive record](https://archive.org/details/structureofsingi0000mill)
- Miller's book opens with "The Coordinated Onset and Release", then "The Supported Singing Voice" (appoggio), then "Agility in Singing". So onset comes before breath management and agility in his sequence (background knowledge, not re-verified this session). — [Internet Archive record](https://archive.org/details/structureofsingi0000mill)
- Miller holds that appoggio (breath management) "can be developed through sostenuto exercises in combination with onset exercises without lengthy explanations". In other words, breath is trained through sung exercises rather than breathing lectures. — [Scribd copy of Miller, via search summary](https://www.scribd.com/document/465276251/The-Structure-of-Singing-Richard-Miller-pdf)
- James McKinney's *The Diagnosis and Correction of Vocal Faults* (1982) uses a medical-style model: symptoms, then causes, then corrective procedures. It has sections on breathing/support faults and phonation faults, and classifies basic phonation as breathy, tight (tense) or balanced. — [Google Books](https://books.google.com/books/about/The_diagnosis_correction_of_vocal_faults.html?id=EHAJAQAAMAAJ); [vocalpedagogy.com summary](https://vocalpedagogy.com/vocal-pedagogue/james-mckinney/)

**Evidence-informed / voice science [E]/[P]**
- Ingo Titze's five "best" warm-ups progress as follows: (1) lip trills, tongue trills, humming or straw phonation, to activate respiration and improve fold closure; (2) two-octave glides on /i/ or /u/, to stretch the folds; (3) /a/–/i/ scales with tongue rolls, to loosen tongue and jaw; (4) messa di voce, to regulate closure and airflow; (5) staccato arpeggios. — [Titze, "The Five Best Vocal Warm-Up Exercises" (NCVS/Utah PDF)](https://vocology.utah.edu/_resources/documents/titzes_top_five_vocal_warm_ups.pdf); [Matt Edwards summary](https://edwardsvoice.wordpress.com/2015/10/17/mix-it-up-monday-dr-ingo-titzes-five-favorite-vocal-warm-ups-for-singers/)
- Titze's rationale for semi-occluded vocal tract (SOVT) work (J Speech Lang Hear Res, 2006) is that the straw plus vocal tract adds inertance. This lowers phonation threshold pressure and helps the folds sustain self-oscillation. — [Titze 2006, "Voice Training and Therapy With a Semi-Occluded Vocal Tract"](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/7108540_Voice_Training_and_Therapy_With_a_Semi-Occluded_Vocal_Tract_Rationale_and_Scientific_Underpinnings)

**CCM / contemporary pedagogy [P]**
- Somatic Voicework (LoVetri) aims at healthy function "in any style" (pop, rock, jazz, music theatre, classical). Its exercises are "focused on function… what the voice is doing, not just how the voice sounds". — [Baldwin Wallace LoVetri Institute](https://cas.bw.edu/departments/teacher-education/lovetri-institute/); [somaticvoicework.com](https://somaticvoicework.com/)
- LoVetri principle: isolate head (Mode 2) and chest (Mode 1) registers before building a music-theatre mix. Establish chest at the bottom of the range and head at the top, and have them "cross" in the middle. — [LoVetri Institute strategies page (via search)](https://somaticvoicework.com/strategies/); [NATS Music Theater resources](https://www.nats.org/Music_Theater_-_Resources.html)
- A 2025/26 comparative analysis describes Estill Voice Training as demanding high anatomical awareness and "conscious management of laryngeal structures (isolated muscle control)". It describes Speech Level Singing as "speech-based natural coordination and relaxation… an indirect and perceptual process" aimed at reducing tension across register transitions. It also covers Complete Vocal Technique. — [ResearchGate: Vocal control in voice pedagogy: EVT vs SLS vs CVT](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/404466632_Vocal_control_in_voice_pedagogy_A_comparative_analysis_of_Estill_Voice_Training_Speech_Level_Singing_and_Complete_Vocal_Technique)
- Studio blogs claim SLS is "better for beginners" because it is fast to learn and focuses on connecting registers. This is **[M]**: studio marketing, no outcome data. — [Ensemble Schools](https://www.ensembleschools.com/the-inside-voice/speech-level-singing-vs-estill-voice-training/); [Toronto Arts Academy](https://torontoartsacademy.com/vocal-technique-speech-level-singing-sls-vs-estill-voice-training-evt/)

**Published exam curricula (Australia/UK) [P]**
- AMEB Preliminary Singing: one vocalise as technical work, three songs, then aural tests and general-knowledge questions. — [AMEB Singing Preliminary (Classical)](https://www.ameb.edu.au/singing-preliminary-classical); [AMEB NSW Singing](https://www.ameb.nsw.edu.au/subjects/instruments/singing)
- AMEB Grade 1 Singing (Comprehensive): pieces, required technical work, aural tests, sight-reading and general knowledge. — [AMEB NSW Singing Comprehensive](https://www.ameb.nsw.edu.au/subjects/instruments/singing-comprehensive/singing-comprehensive-preliminary); [AMEB Grade 1 Classical](https://www.ameb.edu.au/singing-grade-1-classical)
- AMEB Singing for Leisure (the contemporary stream) has a Technical Workbook with fully notated scales, arpeggios and technical exercises from Preliminary to Grade 8. — [Arties Music: AMEB Singing for Leisure Technical Workbook](https://artiesmusiconline.com.au/products/ameb-singing-for-leisure-technical-workbook); [AMEB Singing for Leisure Preliminary](https://www.ameb.edu.au/singing-for-leisure-preliminary-for-leisure)
- Trinity College London teaches technique through "mini songs" (Vocal Exercises Initial–Grade 8). Each targets one technical focus: pitching accuracy, dynamic gradation, rhythmic precision or diction. Candidates may instead choose a Vaccai exercise or an unaccompanied folk song, and perform from memory. — [Trinity: Exploring Trinity's Vocal Exercises](https://www.trinitycollege.com/qualifications/music/grade-exams/singing/resources/singing-technical-work/exploring-trinitys-new-vocal-exercises); [Trinity: Technical work in singing exams](https://resources.trinitycollege.com/singing/technical-work-in-trinitys-singing-exams)

**Breath management: where tradition and evidence diverge [E]**
- Salomoni, van den Hoorn & Hodges (PLOS One, 2016) used objective kinematics to compare classical singers with untrained people. Singers used a greater abdominal contribution to lung volume and more rib-cage/abdomen asynchrony, with consistent pre-phonatory **inward** abdominal-wall movement. Inter-singer variability was high. — [PLOS One 2016](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0155084)
- A listener-perception study of abdominal kinematic directives in female classical singers found no direct, proportional link between respiratory behaviour and standard of singing. It also did not support the assumption that an "abdomen-outward" strategy harms vocal quality. — [PubMed 20456913 (J Voice)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20456913/)
- A secondary (non-peer-reviewed) summary says classical singers start phrases at 70–100% of vital capacity versus about 60% in speech. It also advises that, given variability among elite singers, teachers should "guide individual discovery" rather than impose one breathing pattern. — [Voice Science: Appoggio](https://www.voicescience.org/2025/11/lexicon/appoggio/)
- A 2024 Journal of Voice study examined how body type affects respiratory kinematics in female classical singers, another sign that breathing patterns vary by individual. — [ScienceDirect: Effect of Body Type on Respiratory Kinematics](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0892199724001644)

### Inferences
- The shared sequence across traditions for months 0–12 looks like this. First, comfortable alignment and low-effort breathing. Then onset/phonation balance (not breathy, not pressed), using SOVT and "HA"/thought-h onsets. Then pitch accuracy and simple scales in the comfortable middle range. Then vowel shaping and resonance. Then the register bridge (sirens/glides, descending head-voice patterns, light mix). Legato, dynamics (messa di voce) and agility/staccato are layered on top, with articulation/diction through songs. Classical teachers put onset/appoggio first. CCM teachers put register isolation and balance first. Both use SOVT early.
- The older "belly out / push down" breath-support dogma is not supported as a universal rule and is effectively superseded by "individual, efficient coordination". A self-guided app should avoid prescriptive abdominal instructions.
- Exam curricula (AMEB, Trinity) at Preliminary/Initial level assess mostly musical outcomes: pitching, rhythm, dynamics, diction and songs, plus one vocalise. They do not examine explicit technique, which fits an app built around songs plus short technical exercises.

### Gaps
- I could not access full text for Miller's, McKinney's or LoVetri's recommended week-by-week sequencing. No source gave a month-by-month curriculum for adult beginners.
- No sources were retrieved on Kodály choral practice (solfège/hand signs) for adult beginners, or on posture/Alexander Technique evidence in singers.
- No peer-reviewed comparison of outcomes between pedagogical methods (EVT vs SLS vs CVT vs classical) for beginners was found. Claims about which is "best for beginners" are marketing.

## 2. What exercises do teachers actually use at each stage, and what does each train (vowels, consonants, range)?

### Takeaway
A small, well-agreed toolkit covers most beginner technique. It includes SOVT (lip/tongue trills, humming, straw phonation) for efficient, low-effort phonation, and glides/sirens on /u/ or /i/ for range and register blending. It adds 5-note scales and arpeggios for pitch and agility, "HA"/thought-h onsets for balanced onset, descending light head-voice patterns on [i]/[u] for registration, messa di voce for dynamics and breath control, and staccato arpeggios for onset precision and agility. Evidence for SOVT is the strongest of these, yet it is still mostly short-term and from clinical or trained populations.

### Cited Findings
**Exercise-by-exercise toolkit**
- **Lip trill / tongue trill / hum / straw phonation (SOVT).** Trains respiration activation, better fold closure and lower phonation threshold pressure. Used first in warm-ups (Titze). — [Titze Five Best](https://vocology.utah.edu/_resources/documents/titzes_top_five_vocal_warm_ups.pdf); [Titze 2006](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/7108540_Voice_Training_and_Therapy_With_a_Semi-Occluded_Vocal_Tract_Rationale_and_Scientific_Underpinnings) [P]/[E]
- **Lip trill through the break.** Teacher resources claim that "the vibrating lips absorb excess air pressure", reducing the laryngeal effort that causes a crack at the passaggio. This mechanism is plausible and consistent with SOVT theory, but it is stated by a commercial site, not tested. — [vocalrangetester.com: Vocal breaks](https://vocalrangetester.com/vocal-breaks/) [M/P]
- **Descending trill-to-vowel patterns.** A beginning-teacher guide describes rapid five-note patterns with the first notes tongue- or lip-trilled and the last five on a vowel. They start above the passaggio and move down by key. — [Rider University: A Basic Guide to Vocal Exercises for Beginning Singing Teachers (via search)](https://www.rider.edu/sites/default/files/files/voiceteacher-basic_guide_for_beginning_teachers.pdf) [P]
- **Glides / sirens.** Two-octave glides on /i/ or /u/ to stretch the folds and connect registers. — [Titze Five Best](https://vocology.utah.edu/_resources/documents/titzes_top_five_vocal_warm_ups.pdf) [P]
- **/a/–/i/ scales with tongue rolls.** Loosen tongue and jaw. — [Titze Five Best](https://vocology.utah.edu/_resources/documents/titzes_top_five_vocal_warm_ups.pdf) [P]
- **Messa di voce** (swell and diminish on one pitch). Regulates closure and airflow "with finesse". This is the classic dynamics/breath-control exercise. — [Titze Five Best](https://vocology.utah.edu/_resources/documents/titzes_top_five_vocal_warm_ups.pdf) [P]
- **Staccato arpeggios.** Clean, repeated onsets and agility. — [Titze Five Best](https://vocology.utah.edu/_resources/documents/titzes_top_five_vocal_warm_ups.pdf) [P]
- **Onset exercises (Miller).** (a) "HA, HA, HA, HA, HA", slow and deliberate as one phrase, lingering on the aspirated [h] so the singer feels breath over the folds followed by sound. (b) Balanced onset: "AH, AH, AH, AH", thinking a slight /h/ before each vowel without letting it be heard, with "no sensation of breath expulsion" before the tone. — [Miller, Structure of Singing (via search summary)](https://www.scribd.com/document/465276251/The-Structure-of-Singing-Richard-Miller-pdf); [Richard Miller handout](https://pdfcoffee.com/richard-miller-handout-4-pdf-free.html) [P]
- **Head-register development (LoVetri).** Best vowels are [i] "ee" and [u] "oo", at soft-to-moderate volume on medium-high pitches. Example: a 5-note **descending** scale on [i] starting on C5, moving down by half-steps, "as lightly as possible" to keep a head-dominant sound. (C5 is for treble voices; men would work an octave lower or in falsetto.) — [LoVetri strategies (via search)](https://somaticvoicework.com/strategies/) [P]
- **Vowel modification / copertura.** As pitch rises, vowels open slightly toward a more neutral, rounded shape: "ah"→"uh", "ay"→"eh"→"uh", "ee"→"ih". This eases the register transition while the listener still hears the same vowel. — [SingWise: How to eliminate register breaks](https://www.singwise.com/articles/how-to-eliminate-register-breaks-and-develop-evenness-of-scale); [Katrina Pfitzner: Passaggio playbook](https://katrinapfitzner.com/passaggio-playbook/) [P]

**Evidence base for these exercises [E]**
- A Journal of Voice scoping review (2025) of 20 studies on vocal exercises in **trained** singers covered SOVT, vocal function exercises and structured warm-ups. It reported acoustic, aerodynamic and perceptual benefits and called for RCTs to establish efficacy. — [J Voice scoping review 2025](https://www.jvoice.org/article/S0892-1997(25)00395-9/abstract); [PubMed 41058356](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41058356/)
- A systematic review and meta-analysis (J Voice 2021) examined SOVT in patients with dysphonia. SOVT evidence is strongest in clinical populations. — [ScienceDirect: SOVTE meta-analysis](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0892199721001958)
- A Journal of Voice study compared traditional singing warm-up with SOVT warm-up on acoustic parameters of the singing voice. (Direction of effect not visible in the snippet.) — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0892199714002768)
- Laryngeal (EGG-type) evidence in professionally trained sopranos found four vocal-fold patterns across the first and second passaggi: smooth transitions with increasing or decreasing glottal-closure duration, abrupt transitions, and intermediate loss of fold contact. Register transitions therefore vary even among professionals. — [PMC5414960](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5414960/)
- A 2025 study examined SOVT and self-efficacy/motivation in female adolescent singers. This was a motivational outcome, not an acoustic one. — [Voice and Speech Review 2025](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/23268263.2025.2588083)

**Pitch-accuracy training exercises [E]**
- Pfordresher & Greenspon (Musicae Scientiae 2025, vol. 29, pp. 240–255) tested whether training inaccurate singers across a **wide** pitch range (one octave) beat a **narrow** range (a perfect fifth, 7 semitones). The rationale: inaccurate singing reflects impoverished sensorimotor mapping, one sign of which is a restricted vocal pitch range during matching that is "not reducible to physical vocal limitations". I could not access the result. — [SAGE: Effects of pitch range on singing accuracy training](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/10298649241289542)

### Inferences
- A beginner stage map consistent with these sources:
  - Weeks 1–4: SOVT (lip trill/hum/straw) slides and 3–5-note patterns in the middle range; pitch matching; "HA" onsets; relaxed sustained notes.
  - Months 2–3: 5-note scales and arpeggios on /i/ /e/ /a/ /o/ /u/; sirens/glides on /u/ and lip trill through the register break; staccato on arpeggios; light descending head-voice patterns.
  - Months 3–6: octave scales, messa di voce on comfortable middle pitches, legato phrases on vowels then with text, simple vowel modification near the passaggio.
  - Months 6–12: extend range gradually from the middle outward, practise mix/bridging, dynamics in songs, agility.
- **Descending** patterns that start in head voice are a consistent CCM and classical strategy to stop beginners pulling chest voice up. Most beginner apps (and most of Note by Note's current lessons) use **ascending** patterns from the bottom.
- Vowels matter. Closed vowels /i/ /u/ plus SOVT are used for ease and head voice. /a/ is used later for openness, with modification up high.

### Gaps
- The result of the Pfordresher & Greenspon (2025) wide vs narrow range training could not be retrieved.
- No study was found comparing the effectiveness of specific beginner exercises (e.g., lip trill vs straw vs hum) in **untrained adults**. The SOVT literature is mostly trained singers or dysphonic patients.
- Exact Trinity Initial/Grade 1 and AMEB Preliminary vocalise content (notes, vowels) was not retrievable.

## 3. Which aspects of technique can an app plausibly teach and assess from a phone mic, and which need a human ear?

### Takeaway
Fundamental frequency (pitch) from a phone mic is reliable, and so is anything derived from it: pitch accuracy in cents, precision/consistency, scooping, drift, flat/sharp tendency, vibrato rate and extent, glide continuity and register "cracks" as pitch jumps. Duration and timing are also reliable. Relative loudness is usable, but absolute dB is not calibrated. Voice-quality measures (breathiness, strain, CPP, singer's formant, resonance, onset type) degrade heavily with room noise and device differences. They need a human ear, or should at most be coarse, low-stakes cues. Real-time visual pitch feedback has decent evidence of helping beginners. Motor-learning research warns that constant feedback can hurt retention.

### Cited Findings
**Real-time visual feedback (RTVF) for pitch [E]**
- Wilson, Lee, Callaghan & Thorpe (J Interdisciplinary Music Studies, 2008) had singers of varied experience sing interval patterns with RTVF (Sing & See). Beginning singers benefited from "the richness of the contextual feedback", but skilled singers' pitch accuracy **decreased** because of extra cognitive load. — [ResearchGate: Learning to sing in tune: Does real-time visual feedback help?](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/251823345_Learning_to_sing_in_tune_Does_real-time_visual_feedback_help)
- Sing & See research summaries (vendor-hosted, reporting the authors' academic studies) state that RTVF subjects improved pitch accuracy after a single lesson, whereas no-feedback controls did not. Pitch errors fell by up to 15 Hz from baseline to post-test. Singers' accuracy was **worse during** the lesson, while watching the screen, than before or after. — [Sing & See research](https://www.singandsee.com/singandsee_research); [academia.edu: Does real-time visual feedback improve pitch accuracy in singing?](https://www.academia.edu/1870455/Does_real_time_visual_feedback_improve_pitch_accuracy_in_singing) [E, vendor-hosted]
- Another adult study found that participants with **previous** singing training benefited more from visual feedback than untrained ones. This conflicts with Wilson et al. 2008; the snippet does not make clear which study it came from. — [ResearchGate: The effect of concurrent visual feedback on adult singing accuracy](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/334113826_The_effect_of_concurrent_visual_feedback_on_adult_singing_accuracy); contradicted by [Wilson et al. 2008](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/251823345_Learning_to_sing_in_tune_Does_real-time_visual_feedback_help)
- An adult poor-pitch remediation study randomly assigned participants to three 20-minute conditions: visual feedback with auditory doubling, auditory feedback alone, or a speech-imitation control. It used an app (Erol Singing Studio) for pitch matching. — [ResearchGate: Visual and Auditory Feedback on Adult Poor-Pitch Remediation](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/352064762_The_Effect_of_Visual_and_Auditory_Feedback_on_Adult_Poor-Pitch_Remediation); [app screenshot figure](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/A-screenshot-from-the-Erol-Singing-Studio-app-demonstrating-a-single-pitch-matching_fig1_352064762)
- A 2022 review (Applied Sciences 12(21):10781) concludes that RTVF "enhances vocal pitch accuracy in singing lessons", with teachers reporting better engagement and communication. It notes that lab tools (EGG, inverse filtering, subglottal pressure, respiratory kinematics) can show phonation type, voice breaks and breathing. Those are not available from a phone mic. — [MDPI: Real-Time Visual Feedback in Singing Pedagogy](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/12/21/10781)
- Frontiers in Psychology (2021) compared visual and auditory feedback in violin and singing-voice pitch matching. — [Frontiers 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.684693/full)

**Motor-learning caution about feedback frequency [E/P]**
- "Principles of Motor Learning in Classical Singing Teaching" (J Voice) describes the **guidance hypothesis**: feedback on every trial makes learners rely on augmented feedback instead of their own sensory feedback, so performance drops when it is removed. Low-frequency knowledge of results and high contextual interference (mixed practice) hurt short-term acquisition but **improve long-term retention**. — [ScienceDirect: Principles of Motor Learning in Classical Singing Teaching](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0892199717306094); [ResearchGate copy](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/330810015_Principles_of_Motor_Learning_in_Classical_Singing_Teaching)
- A study examined practice structure and feedback frequency in voice motor learning in older adults. (Result not visible in the snippet.) — [PubMed 37210322](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37210322/)
- Singing pedagogy literature (Journal of Singing "Mindful Voice" series) discusses internal vs external locus of attention in voice training. — [ResearchGate: Motor Learning and Voice Training Part II](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/323839558_MINDFUL_VOICE_Motor_Learning_and_Voice_Training_Part_II_Locus_of_Attention_Internal_or_External_That_is_the_Question)

**Smartphone measurement validity [E]**
- Several studies conclude that iPhone and Android recordings can support F0, jitter, shimmer, NHR, CPP and AVQI analysis. Others find that **only F0 is reliably robust**, with other measures sensitive to device and conditions, and "there is still a lack of clear consensus". — [J Voice lit review: Smartphone use in clinical voice recording](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S089219971930284X); [JSLHR 2024: Validity of acoustic measures from smartphones](https://doi.org/10.1044/2024_jslhr-23-00759); [J Voice 2023: Smartphone recordings comparable to gold standard](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0892199723000310)
- Noise strongly reduces CPP: it fell significantly from a sound booth to every exam-room noise condition tested. — [ERIC/JSLHR 2020: Acoustic analyses in nonoptimized conditions](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1281415); [PubMed 33186510](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33186510/)
- AVQI captured with a smartphone was tested with added ambient noise (J Voice 2021). — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0892199721000734)

**Onset measurement [E]**
- The three onset types are aspirate (breathy), simultaneous (balanced) and glottal (hard). Acoustically, an aspirate onset shows pre-voicing noise and a glottal onset an abrupt transient. Vocal rise time (VRT) is the standard acoustic metric. — [Voice Science: Phonatory onset types](https://www.voicescience.org/lexicon/phonatory-onset-types/); [JASA: Vocal rise time and perception of hard glottal attack](https://pubs.aip.org/asa/jasa/article/106/4_Supplement/2239/546965/Vocal-rise-time-and-perception-of-a-hard-glottal)
- A 2026 automated VRT study (VOAT; 146 adult females, four lab-quality datasets of sustained /a/ onsets) found automated measurement was least accurate for **hard** onsets, with mean absolute error up to 71.0 ms, even on lab recordings. — [MDPI Bioengineering 2026: VOAT](https://www.mdpi.com/2306-5354/13/8/950); [PMC13509588](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13509588/)

**Vibrato [E/secondary]**
- Vibrato is typically 4.5–6.5 Hz (reported means 4–7 Hz) with about 50–120 cents extent. Under about 20 cents is heard as straight tone, and over about 150 cents as excessive. — [Voice Science: Vibrato](https://www.voicescience.org/lexicon/vibrato/); [Voice Science: Vibrato extent](https://www.voicescience.org/lexicon/vibrato-extent/) (secondary summary); [J Voice multicenter study of vibrato rate/extent in college music majors](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0892199715002064)

**Recent automatic singing-mistake detection [E, preprint]**
- A February 2026 arXiv preprint addresses automatic detection and analysis of singing mistakes for music pedagogy. — [arXiv 2602.06917](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.06917)

### Inferences
- **Reliably measurable on an iPhone:**
  - F0, and so cents error (accuracy).
  - Spread across repeats (precision).
  - Pitch drift over a held note.
  - Scooping: approach from below in the first 100–300 ms.
  - Overshoot.
  - Glide continuity: a register "crack" shows up as a sudden F0 jump or dropout during a siren.
  - Vibrato rate and extent (5 Hz oscillation is easy to detect).
  - Note duration, onset timing relative to the beat, phrase length and maximum phonation time.
- **Relative-only:** loudness (crescendo/decrescendo shape in messa di voce, consistency), because phone mic gain and distance are uncalibrated.
- **Unreliable, human-ear or lab territory:** breathiness vs pressed phonation, onset type (VRT is error-prone even in the lab), resonance/"ring", nasality, jaw/tongue tension, posture, larynx height, register quality (chest vs head timbre), and strain/vocal fatigue.
- Visual pitch feedback helps beginners learn, but accuracy while watching can be worse than accuracy afterwards. The app's score should come from **no-look** trials, and feedback should fade as the learner improves.

### Gaps
- No study was found validating consumer-app pitch detection specifically for **singing** (as opposed to sustained vowels) on iPhone Safari/Web Audio.
- The Sing & See "15 Hz" figure lacks the reference pitch in the snippet. At different F0s, 15 Hz corresponds to very different cent values.
- No evidence was found on automatically detecting register (chest/head/mix) from phone audio.

## 4. What are the most common adult beginner faults, and how do teachers correct them in ways a solo learner can follow?

### Takeaway
The recurring faults are:
- breathy (hypo-adducted) or pressed (hyper-adducted) phonation;
- pulling chest voice too high, which leads to cracks, strain and flatness near the top;
- jaw/tongue/neck tension;
- running out of breath or collapsing posture;
- flat singing on high notes and sustained notes;
- scooping into notes;
- believing "I'm tone deaf".

Standard solo-friendly fixes: SOVT exercises, descending light head-voice patterns and sirens through the break, vowel modification up high, "thought-h" onsets, messa di voce and sustained notes for breath, and short, relaxed repetitions. For adults, pitch inaccuracy is usually a trainable sensorimotor (imitation) problem, not deafness.

### Cited Findings
**Pitch inaccuracy / "tone deafness" [E]**
- About 10–15% of the general population sing far from target pitches (inaccuracy). Counting consistency across repeated attempts (**precision**), the proportion with a problem rises to about 55%. — [Frontiers review 2011 (Dalla Bella et al.)](https://frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2011.00164/full); [Pfordresher & Demorest 2021, JRME](https://ubwp.buffalo.edu/apal-pfordresher/wp-content/uploads/sites/79/2022/01/PfordresherDemorest_2021_JRME.pdf)
- In a sample of over 1,000 university students, about 60% judged they could not accurately imitate melodies. In the lab, about 85–90% of occasional singers sing in tune, so adults are unduly pessimistic. — [Frontiers review 2011](https://frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2011.00164/full); [Pfordresher & Brown 2007, Music Perception](http://www.acsu.buffalo.edu/~pqp/pdfs/Pfordresher&Brown_2007_MP.pdf)
- Pfordresher & Brown (2007) showed poor-pitch singing occurs **without** perceptual "tone deafness". It is a vocal pitch **imitation** deficit, a failure of perception-to-motor mapping. This supersedes the older assumption that bad singers can't hear pitch. — [Pfordresher & Brown 2007](http://www.acsu.buffalo.edu/~pqp/pdfs/Pfordresher&Brown_2007_MP.pdf); [PubMed 26029088: deficits of vocal pitch imitation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26029088)
- Tools exist to phenotype poor-pitch singing, e.g. the Sung Performance Battery (Frontiers 2013). — [Frontiers: Sung Performance Battery](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2013.00714/full)
- Auditory imagery ability has been linked to poor-pitch singing. — [GSU: Auditory imagery and the poor-pitch singer](https://neuroscience.gsu.edu/files/2022/12/Auditory-imagery-and-the-poor-pitch-singer.pdf)
- Singing accuracy develops and changes across the lifespan (Pfordresher 2022, Annals NYAS). — [Wiley: Singing accuracy across the lifespan](https://nyaspubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/nyas.14815)

**Faults in the pedagogical literature [P]**
- McKinney's diagnostic clues are audible (breathiness, nasality, vibrato problems, intonation, hoarseness) and visible (postural rigidity, collapsed chest, tight jaw). His principle is "discriminatory hearing assisted by acute observation", in other words a human ear and eye. — [vocalpedagogy.com: McKinney](https://vocalpedagogy.com/vocal-pedagogue/james-mckinney/); [Google Books](https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Diagnosis_and_Correction_of_Vocal_Fa.html?id=znaCDwAAQBAJ)
- Pulling chest voice too high is "one of the most common reasons singers can't stay in tune", creating tension and flatness. Voice teachers commonly teach this. — [John Henny Vocal Studio](https://johnhenny.com/three-reasons-you-are-singing-out-of-tune/) [P/M]
- Flatness on high/sustained notes is attributed to: uneven breath flow; vowels becoming "too dark, heavy or wide" on ah/oh/uh; and over-control creating jaw, tongue and neck tension. — [London Singing Institute: flat on sustained notes](https://www.londonsinginginstitute.co.uk/why-some-singers-go-flat-on-sustained-notes/); [Wyzant expert answer](https://www.wyzant.com/resources/answers/885709/why-do-goes-flat-or-sharp) [P/M]
- A vendor's analysis of 632,000 missed notes from app users reports that flat errors concentrate at the **top** of a singer's range and around the passaggio. **[M]**: vendor data, not peer-reviewed. — [Singing Carrots blog](https://blog.singingcarrots.com/why-do-singers-go-flat-what-632000-missed-notes-taught-us/)

**Corrections a solo learner can follow [P]**
- For breathy vs pressed onset, use Miller's "HA" (feel breath before sound), then the silent "thought-h" before a vowel for a balanced onset. — [Miller via search summary](https://www.scribd.com/document/465276251/The-Structure-of-Singing-Richard-Miller-pdf)
- For register breaks and pushing chest up:
  - Use lip trill through the break. — [vocalrangetester.com](https://vocalrangetester.com/vocal-breaks/)
  - Use descending patterns from head voice on [i]/[u], light and soft. — [LoVetri strategies](https://somaticvoicework.com/strategies/)
  - Modify vowels toward neutral as pitch rises. — [SingWise](https://www.singwise.com/articles/how-to-eliminate-register-breaks-and-develop-evenness-of-scale)
  - Use descending trill-to-vowel patterns started above the passaggio. — [Rider guide](https://www.rider.edu/sites/default/files/files/voiceteacher-basic_guide_for_beginning_teachers.pdf)
- For jaw/tongue tension, use /a/–/i/ scales with tongue rolls (Titze). — [Titze Five Best](https://vocology.utah.edu/_resources/documents/titzes_top_five_vocal_warm_ups.pdf)
- For breath and control, use messa di voce and sustained (sostenuto) singing combined with onset work (Titze; Miller). — [Titze Five Best](https://vocology.utah.edu/_resources/documents/titzes_top_five_vocal_warm_ups.pdf); [Miller](https://www.scribd.com/document/465276251/The-Structure-of-Singing-Richard-Miller-pdf)

**Vocal health factors behind "faults" [E]**
- Systemic hydration improved perceptual and acoustic voice quality in future professional singers (J Voice 2016/17). Dehydration from fasting worsened NHR, shimmer, jitter and s/z ratio. — [J Voice: Effect of hydration on voice quality of future professional vocal performers](https://www.jvoice.org/article/S0892-1997(16)00008-4/abstract); [ASHA CICSD: Effects of Hydration on Voice Acoustics](https://pubs.asha.org/doi/pdf/10.1044/cicsd_36_F_142)
- Fifteen minutes of obligatory mouth breathing (drying the folds) raises phonation threshold pressure and expiratory effort in healthy speakers. Nebulised isotonic saline lowered PTP. Steam improved NHR, shimmer and jitter. — [ResearchGate: Effects of Hydration on Voice Acoustics](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/334394678_Effects_of_Hydration_on_Voice_Acoustics); [West Coast Voice Box summary](https://www.westvoicebox.co.uk/research-blog/hydration)

### Inferences
- The fault an app sees most directly is pitch-related: flat on high notes, drift on sustained notes, scooping, and inconsistency across repeats. All of these can be derived from the pitch trace the app already computes, so results could be broken down **by region of range** and **by position in the note**, not just averaged.
- Faults like breathiness, pressed tone and tension cannot be detected. They need self-check prompts with concrete sensations ("Could you have kept going without squeezing? Is your jaw loose?") and exercises that make the correct coordination the default (SOVT, descending head-voice patterns).
- Telling adult beginners early that most people who think they can't sing actually can, and that precision improves with practice, is evidence-based motivational framing.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed prevalence ranking of technical faults in **adult beginners** was found. Fault lists come from pedagogical texts and teacher blogs.
- No controlled study was found testing whether solo learners can correctly apply imagery-based cues without a teacher.

## 5. How do teachers structure a lesson, and how long and how often should beginners practise?

### Takeaway
The traditional lesson runs warm-up (physical plus vocal, usually starting with SOVT), then technical work, then repertoire, often ending with a cool-down. Evidence supports **short** warm-ups: a few minutes produces perceived vocal ease. Combined physical and vocal warm-ups improve some choral outcomes. Warm-ups improve some acoustic measures, but in one study they did not improve tone-matching accuracy. Beginner practice-length advice (15–30 minutes a day, split into sessions, stop at strain) is teacher and blog consensus with no direct empirical basis. Motor learning favours frequent, spaced, varied practice.

### Cited Findings
**Warm-up evidence [E]**
- McHenry et al. (J Voice) compared specific vs combined warm-ups. Female singers showed a greater reduction in phonation threshold pressure with a combined vocal plus aerobic warm-up. Males showed no difference between vocal-only and vocal-plus-aerobic. — [J Voice: Specific vs combined warm-up strategies](https://www.jvoice.org/article/S0892-1997(08)00004-0/abstract)
- In young female singers, warm-up reduced jitter and shimmer, raised singer's-formant amplitude and improved noise-to-harmonic ratio, **but did not affect tone-matching accuracy**. (The snippet links to a 2023 J Voice paper; the findings match Amir et al.'s earlier work. Exact attribution is uncertain.) — [ScienceDirect: Acoustic analysis of the influence of warm-up on singing voice quality](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0892199723000747)
- University of Miami researchers (J Voice) found that classical singers need only **minutes** of warm-up to perceive vocal ease. — [InventUM (U Miami)](https://news.med.miami.edu/miller-school-researchers-study-ideal-vocal-warm-up-time-for-singers/)
- A combined physical and vocal choral warm-up produced more resonant and more in-tune singing than other procedures (J Voice). — [PubMed 28483445](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28483445/)
- A 2026 study compared the immediate effect of a vocal warm-up with continuous singing on aerodynamic, acoustic and self-assessment measures. (Result not visible.) — [PubMed 41927414](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41927414/)
- A study of warm-up exercises and self-assessed vocal effort exists. (Details not retrieved.) — [PMC10020864](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10020864/)
- Older physiological work (J Voice) asked "What happens during vocal warm-up?" — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0892199705802218)
- Voice-science commentary (December 2025) re-examines whether warm-ups "actually work". — [Voice Science podcast](https://www.voicescience.org/2025/12/podcast/do-vocal-warm-ups-actually-work/)

**Practice length and frequency [M/P]**
- Beginners: 15–30 minutes a day. — [Piano Place Utah](https://thepianoplaceutah.com/optimizing-your-vocal-practice-how-long-should-i-practice-singing-each-day/)
- Alternatively, 20–30 minutes a day, 3–4 times a week, with rest days. — [VoiceLessons.com](https://www.voicelessons.com/blog/how-long-should-you-do-vocal-exercises-guide)
- Split practice through the day because beginners lack stamina. — [LiveAbout](https://www.liveabout.com/how-much-should-i-practice-2994226)
- Warm-ups beyond 15–20 minutes risk fatigue. — [Strawberry Hill Studio](https://www.strawberryhillstudio.online/blog/how-much-voice-training-per-day-is-recommended)
- These are all **[M/P]**: teacher and blog advice, not trials.
- A vendor claims that, from over 100,000 users' data, about 20 minutes a day for 5 weeks gives a "95% chance" of expanding range by 2 notes. It also claims adult beginners show the largest improvements and the biggest gains come in the first weeks. **[M]**: unpublished vendor data, not peer-reviewed. — [Singing Carrots: Can adults learn to sing?](https://blog.singingcarrots.com/can-adults-learn-to-sing/); [Singing Carrots: How long to see improvement](https://singingcarrots.com/blog/how-long-does-it-take-to-see-improvement-from-singing-lessons/)
- Motor-learning principles favour high contextual interference (varied or interleaved practice) and reduced feedback frequency for long-term retention. — [J Voice: Principles of Motor Learning in Classical Singing Teaching](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0892199717306094)

**Exam-syllabus lesson content (Australia) [P]**
- An AMEB Preliminary exam covers a vocalise, three songs, aural tests and general knowledge, which implies lessons cover technique, repertoire and musicianship. — [AMEB Singing Preliminary](https://www.ameb.edu.au/singing-preliminary-classical)

### Inferences
- A defensible daily structure for a self-guided adult beginner:
  - 3–5 minutes of warm-up: body loosening, SOVT slides, gentle glides.
  - 5–10 minutes of technical work: one focus, varied patterns.
  - 5–10 minutes on a song.
  - An optional 1–2 minute gentle descending SOVT cool-down.
- Total 15–25 minutes, most days, with an easy "done for today" stop. Note by Note's roughly 3-minute warm-up is in line with the "minutes suffice" evidence.
- Warm-ups do not by themselves improve pitch matching (per the snippet above). Pitch skill needs targeted practice, which the app's pitch lessons provide.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed dose-response data on practice time for adult beginner singers was found.
- No empirical evidence on vocal cool-downs was retrieved. I did not search for it specifically. Cool-down is tradition, not a tested practice.
- No source quantified typical private-lesson timing (e.g., percentage of a 30-minute lesson on warm-up vs repertoire).

## Implications for a self-guided singing app

These are concrete design implications for Note by Note, each tied to the evidence above. Tiers: [E] empirical, [P] pedagogy, [M] vendor/anecdote, [I] my inference.

1. **Keep the scrolling pitch lane, but score "eyes-off" repetitions and fade the live trace.**
   - Beginners benefit from real-time visual pitch feedback, but accuracy while watching can be worse than afterwards. Too-frequent augmented feedback impairs retention (guidance hypothesis). [E] ([Wilson et al. 2008](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/251823345_Learning_to_sing_in_tune_Does_real-time_visual_feedback_help); [Sing & See](https://www.singandsee.com/singandsee_research); [Motor learning in singing](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0892199717306094))
   - Design: for each exercise, do 1–2 reps with the live trace, then a rep with the trace hidden (show the result only afterwards), then fade the trace as the learner's scores rise. The "echo game" and call-and-response mode already approximate this.
2. **Report precision (consistency across repeats), not just average miss.**
   - Precision problems affect about 55% of people, versus 10–15% with gross inaccuracy. [E] ([Dalla Bella et al. 2011](https://frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2011.00164/full))
   - Design: add the spread (SD) of cents across repeated attempts of the same note to results and to "Copy results for Claude".
3. **Break down results by where in the range and where in the note the error happens.**
   - Flatness concentrates near the top of the range and the passaggio [M/P]. Scooping and drift on sustained notes are classic faults [P].
   - Design: report cents error by range segment (low/middle/high thirds of the singer's tested range). Separately report an onset segment (first ~200 ms: scoop from below or overshoot) and a sustain segment (drift). Send these to the Claude export so the AI coach can map "flat only on high notes" to register/vowel advice. [I]
4. **Don't let the steadiness scoring penalise healthy vibrato.**
   - Vibrato is typically 4.5–6.5 Hz and 50–120 cents. [E/secondary] ([Voice Science vibrato](https://www.voicescience.org/lexicon/vibrato/))
   - Design: compute "hold it steady" scores on pitch smoothed over at least one vibrato cycle (~200–250 ms). Optionally show vibrato rate and extent as an informational (unscored) readout for intermediate learners.
5. **Add SOVT to the daily warm-up and a registration lesson.**
   - Lip trills, hums and straw phonation are consensus first exercises with a mechanistic rationale (lower phonation threshold pressure). [P/E] ([Titze Five Best](https://vocology.utah.edu/_resources/documents/titzes_top_five_vocal_warm_ups.pdf); [Titze 2006](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/7108540_Voice_Training_and_Therapy_With_a_Semi-Occluded_Vocal_Tract_Rationale_and_Scientific_Underpinnings))
   - Design: a 3-minute warm-up of lip trill or hum 5-note slides, then a /u/ siren, then one scale. Hums and trills are voiced, so the pitch lane can still track them, though trill noise may reduce detector confidence. [I] Test detector confidence on lip trills and relax scoring for SOVT steps.
6. **Add descending, light head-voice patterns and "siren through the break" exercises.**
   - Both CCM (LoVetri) and classical practice use descending [i]/[u] patterns from the upper-middle range to keep beginners from dragging chest voice up. [P] ([LoVetri](https://somaticvoicework.com/strategies/); [Rider guide](https://www.rider.edu/sites/default/files/files/voiceteacher-basic_guide_for_beginning_teachers.pdf))
   - Current Note by Note lessons are almost all pitch-accuracy patterns sung from the bottom up.
   - Design: a "Bridging" unit with 5-note descending scales on "oo"/"ee", soft volume, starting a few semitones below the singer's comfortable top. Also sirens on "oo" or lip trill, where the app flags sudden F0 jumps or dropouts as possible register cracks. It should say "try lighter/softer through that spot", not "wrong". [I]
7. **Add an onset lesson using Miller's "HA" then "thought-h" sequence, scored only on what's measurable.**
   - The balanced onset is foundational in classical pedagogy [P] ([Miller](https://www.scribd.com/document/465276251/The-Structure-of-Singing-Richard-Miller-pdf)). Automated onset-type detection is unreliable even in the lab (MAE up to 71 ms for hard onsets). [E] ([VOAT 2026](https://www.mdpi.com/2306-5354/13/8/950))
   - Design: score pitch "arrival" (lands on the note within X ms, no scoop) and repeat consistency. Use self-report prompts for breathiness and hardness ("Did you hear air before the note? Did it 'click'?").
8. **Add dynamics with relative loudness only (messa di voce).**
   - Messa di voce is a core control exercise [P] ([Titze](https://vocology.utah.edu/_resources/documents/titzes_top_five_vocal_warm_ups.pdf)). Phone loudness is uncalibrated. [I]
   - Design: show a crescendo/decrescendo envelope target and score its shape and smoothness while pitch stays steady. Never show absolute dB. Place it on a comfortable middle note only.
9. **Don't try to score tone quality, resonance, breathiness or strain from the phone mic.**
   - Only F0 is robust across devices and rooms. CPP and similar measures collapse in ordinary noise. [E] ([smartphone validity review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S089219971930284X); [noise effect on CPP](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33186510/))
   - Design: use guided self-checks, and consider an optional on-device "listen back" of the last take that is never saved or uploaded. Teachers rely on the ear ([McKinney](https://vocalpedagogy.com/vocal-pedagogue/james-mckinney/)), and self-listening is the closest a solo learner can get. This fits the app's privacy stance if audio stays in memory only. [I]
10. **Treat the range test as "comfortable" range and keep lessons inside it, widening gradually.**
    - Pushing the top is the classic beginner fault [P]. Poor-pitch singers show artificially restricted matching ranges, and training range width is under active study ([Pfordresher & Greenspon 2025](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/10298649241289542), result not accessed). [E]
    - Design: have the range test ask for the highest note that's easy, not the highest squeak. Set lesson ceilings 2–3 semitones below the tested maximum, and add a later "stretch" option.
11. **Replace breathing dogma with light, individual cues and measurable breath tasks.**
    - Empirical work does not support one prescribed abdominal pattern, and variability among good singers is high. [E] ([PLOS One 2016](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0155084); [PubMed 20456913](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20456913/))
    - Design: in "Breath and control", time sustained notes and phrases (maximum phonation time, phrase completion) and track them over weeks. Give cues like "breathe low and easy, don't lift the shoulders", not "push the belly out".
12. **Structure each daily session as warm-up, then one technical focus, then a song, then an optional cool-down, capped at about 15–25 minutes.**
    - A few minutes of warm-up suffice [E] ([U Miami](https://news.med.miami.edu/miller-school-researchers-study-ideal-vocal-warm-up-time-for-singers/)). Beginner practice advice is 15–30 minutes with rest [M/P]. Warm-up alone doesn't improve tone-matching [E] ([J Voice warm-up acoustics](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0892199723000747)).
    - Design: keep the 3-minute warm-up. Add a gentle "that's enough for today" message after about 25 minutes, and a stop if hoarse/strain message. Add a hydration tip, since systemic hydration improves voice measures [E] ([J Voice hydration](https://www.jvoice.org/article/S0892-1997(16)00008-4/abstract)).
13. **Interleave exercise types within sessions instead of blocking one pattern many times.**
    - High contextual interference improves long-term retention. [E/P] ([Motor learning in singing](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0892199717306094))
    - Design: the daily warm-up or a "review" mode could shuffle short items from different lessons (a leap, a 5-note scale, a hold, a siren).
14. **Embed technique inside songs, Trinity-style, and label each song's technical focus.**
    - Trinity's "mini songs" embed one technical focus each, and AMEB Preliminary pairs one vocalise with three songs. [P] ([Trinity](https://www.trinitycollege.com/qualifications/music/grade-exams/singing/resources/singing-technical-work/exploring-trinitys-new-vocal-exercises); [AMEB](https://www.ameb.edu.au/singing-preliminary-classical))
    - Design: tag songs by focus, e.g. Amazing Grace for legato and long phrases, Row Row Row Your Boat for diction and rhythm, Ode to Joy for stepwise pitching, Waltzing Matilda for range. Show which technique each song practises. Optionally note alignment with AMEB Singing for Leisure Preliminary expectations for Australian users who might later sit exams.
15. **Use evidence-based encouragement about "tone deafness".**
    - About 60% of adults think they can't sing in tune, but 85–90% can. Poor pitch is a trainable imitation problem, not deafness. [E] ([Pfordresher & Brown 2007](http://www.acsu.buffalo.edu/~pqp/pdfs/Pfordresher&Brown_2007_MP.pdf); [Dalla Bella et al. 2011](https://frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2011.00164/full))
    - Design: say this on the onboarding/range screen. Keep call-and-response ("hear then sing"), since it directly trains the perception-to-production mapping that poor-pitch singers lack. [I]
16. **Enrich "Copy results for Claude" with fault-relevant signals.**
    - A chatbot coach can only reason from what's exported. [I]
    - Design: include precision (SD), error by range third, scoop/overshoot rate, drift on holds, crack events during sirens, longest comfortable hold, session length, and self-check answers (breathy? strain? jaw?). With these, Claude can apply McKinney-style symptom → cause → correction reasoning ([McKinney](https://vocalpedagogy.com/vocal-pedagogue/james-mckinney/)).
