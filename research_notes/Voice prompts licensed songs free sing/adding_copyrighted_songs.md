*Researched 1 October 2026. This is not legal advice. Where it matters, the owner should confirm with APRA AMCOS (02 9935 7900, licensing@apra.com.au) or the Arts Law Centre of Australia.*

## 1. Which rights does the app touch when it includes a song, and does a private or family-use exception help?

### Takeaway
A song is usually two copyrights: the melody (a musical work) and the lyrics (a literary work). Note by Note copies both into `songs.js`, arranges them (its own synth piano part, transposed into each child's key), shows the lyrics in time with the music, and makes all of this available online through a public site and a public repo. In Australian terms that is reproduction, adaptation/arrangement and communication to the public. No private or domestic exception covers it, because Australia has no general fair use and its private-copying exceptions are narrow and format-specific. It makes no difference that only the family uses the app: anything on a public GitHub Pages site or in a public repo is made available to the whole world.

### Cited Findings
- [FACT] Under the Copyright Act 1968 (Cth), "communicate" means to "make available online or electronically transmit" a work. University copyright guides describe putting material on the internet as communicating it to the public. — [Victoria University copyright definitions](https://libraryguides.vu.edu.au/VUCopyright/definitions); [Copyright Act 1968, compilation dated 2 April 2026](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C1968A00063/2026-04-02/2026-04-02/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html) (could not fetch the Act text directly)
- [FACT] The exclusive rights include copying (reproducing), publishing, performing in public and communicating the work. — [University of Tasmania, Copyright law overview](https://www.utas.edu.au/copyright/copyright-law)
- [FACT] APRA AMCOS licences do not cover lyric changes or arrangements of musical works. Those need permission directly from the copyright owner, usually the music publisher. — [APRA AMCOS FAQs](https://www.apraamcos.com.au/about/help/faqs); [APRA AMCOS NZ FAQs](https://www.apraamcos.co.nz/about/help/faqs)
- [FACT] APRA AMCOS's church guide says no AMCOS licence permits churches to reproduce lyrics. For lyric reproduction you must get permission from the copyright owner (usually the publisher), and APRA AMCOS will supply the publisher's contact details. — [APRA AMCOS Music Copyright Guide for Churches (hosted by Broken Bay diocese)](https://www.bbcatholic.org.au/ArticleDocuments/1566/Music-Copyright-Guide-for-Churches%20-%20APRA-AMCOS.pdf.aspx); [APRA AMCOS Guide to Music Copyright for Instrumental Teachers](https://assets.apraamcos.com.au/images/PDFs/Music-Licences/Guide-to-Music-Copyright-for-Teachers.pdf)
- [FACT] Lyric companies sell "Lyric Display" (full lyrics) and "Lyric Synchronization" (line-by-line or word-by-word timed lyrics) as separate products. The industry treats timed sing-along lyrics as distinct from static display. — [ProgrammableWeb: LyricFind API](https://www.programmableweb.com/api/lyricfind)
- [FACT] Australia has no general "fair use" exception. — [Copyright Agency: exceptions](https://www.copyright.com.au/about-copyright/exceptions/); [ALRC Discussion Paper 79, private and domestic use](https://www.alrc.gov.au/publication/copyright-and-the-digital-economy-dp-79/9-private-and-domestic-use/fair-use-2/)
- [FACT] The private-use exceptions are format shifting (introduced 2007) and time shifting. Format shifting lets the owner of a legitimate copy of books, newspapers and periodicals, photographs, videotapes or sound recordings make a copy in another format for private and domestic use (e.g. ss 43C, 109A, 110AA). Those copies cannot be sold, given to others or used in public. — [ALRC DP79, current law](https://www.alrc.gov.au/publication/copyright-and-the-digital-economy-dp-79/9-private-and-domestic-use/current-law-2/); [University of Melbourne, personal use](https://copyright.unimelb.edu.au/shared/using-copyright-material/personal-use); [Queensland Law Handbook, exceptions](https://queenslandlawhandbook.org.au/the-queensland-law-handbook/your-rights-and-responsibilities/copyright-and-moral-rights/exceptions-to-copyright-infringement/)
- [FACT] **Recent (October 2025):** on 26 or 27 October 2025 the Attorney-General (Michelle Rowland) ruled out a text-and-data-mining exception. A broader fair dealing or fair use change was considered and not adopted. Further consultation is on AI licensing models, not on fair use. — [APRA AMCOS news: TDM response](https://www.apraamcos.com.au/about-us/news-and-events/tdm-response); [Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/pro/australia-government-shuts-down-copyright-exemptions-ai/); [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=6e4e8b4d-d131-4754-bac9-8ba2dabc3eb6)
- [FACT] GitHub handles copyright complaints under the US DMCA. It cannot disable single files, so it contacts the repository owner and gives about **1 business day** to remove or change the named content before disabling access. Notices are published in a public repo (github/dmca). — [GitHub DMCA Takedown Policy](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/content-removal-policies/dmca-takedown-policy); [github/dmca repository](https://github.com/github/dmca)
- [FACT] Rights-holders do enforce Australian children's song copyrights. Larrikin Music, owner of "Kookaburra Sits in the Old Gum Tree", went looking for unlicensed uses (including flute charts) and won against "Down Under" (Federal Court 2010, Full Court appeal dismissed 31 March 2011, High Court special leave refused October 2011). — [WIPO Lex: EMI Songs v Larrikin [2011] FCAFC 47](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/judgments/details/692); [Songfacts: Kookaburra](https://www.songfacts.com/facts/marion-sinclair/kookaburra); [Davies Collison Cave](https://dcc.com/news-and-insights/full-court-decides-on-appeal-that-a-substantial-part-of-kookaburra-was/)

### Inferences
- **Rights touched by a copyrighted song in the app as built today** (my analysis; check with Arts Law):
  1. *Reproduction* of the musical work (melody encoded as note data) and the literary work (lyrics typed as syllable tokens) in `songs.js`, in the git history, and in every phone's service-worker cache.
  2. *Adaptation or arrangement*: the app's synth piano accompaniment and per-child transposition. Transposing alone is arguably not an "arrangement", but an added accompaniment is closer to one. APRA AMCOS says arrangements fall outside its licences either way.
  3. *Communication to the public*: the site is open to anyone with the URL, and the repo is open to anyone at all. In Australian law "the public" is generally read as the copyright owner's public, so a page open to the world counts even if only five people ever visit it. I could not fetch case law on this, so confirm.
  4. *Public performance*: the children singing at home is almost certainly not "in public", so this right is not engaged. I found no source to cite; this is general understanding.
  5. *Synchronisation*: in the strict sense this means putting music into film or video, which the app does not do. Timed lyric display is licensed commercially as "lyric synchronisation" (see LyricFind), so a publisher would expect a sync-style lyric licence for the sing-along view.
  6. *Sound recording (master) rights*: none today, because the app synthesises its own audio. They would arise only if the app played someone's recording (an MP3, or YouTube audio it extracted).
- **US angle:** GitHub Pages and GitHub are US services, so US law and the DMCA apply to the hosting. A US rights-holder, or an Australian publisher with US rights, would normally send GitHub a DMCA notice rather than sue. The realistic worst case for a hobby app is a takedown notice published in github/dmca. A damages claim is possible but unlikely for a non-commercial family app. That is inference, not a legal assessment.
- **Public repo:** the song data in a public repo is a separate copy and distribution channel from the site. Even if the site were somehow restricted, a public repo containing copyrighted lyrics and melodies is the plainest form of making them available.
- **Is there any family-use exception?** No. None of the format-shifting exceptions covers typing lyrics or transcribing a melody, and none applies to anything placed online. Fair dealing for "research or study" (s 40) is the only arguable fit, for a child practising a song. It is untested for this use, and it would not cover publishing the material on a public website.

### Gaps
- I could not fetch the Act or any case law on what "to the public" means for a website that is public but little used. This needs Arts Law or a lawyer to confirm.
- I could not confirm whether GitHub Pages can be served from a private repository on a free plan, or whether the Pages site could be access-restricted on the owner's plan. My understanding is that private-repo Pages need a paid plan and the site stays public anyway. Not verified this session.

## 2. Licensing bodies and costs: APRA AMCOS, lyric providers (LyricFind, Musixmatch), and how commercial singing apps license their catalogues

### Takeaway
No affordable licence covers what Note by Note does. The APRA AMCOS **Online Mini** licence is cheap (from A$275 a year incl. GST per category of use for services earning under A$25,000 a year per activity) and has karaoke and backing-track categories. However, APRA AMCOS says lyric reproduction and arrangements fall outside its licences, so lyric display and synth arrangements would still need permission from each publisher. The lyric APIs (Musixmatch, LyricFind) do not publish prices. Their free tiers give only 30% previews, and commercial and synced-lyric access is negotiated. Commercial singing and karaoke apps get their catalogues through direct publisher and record-label deals, and drop songs when those deals lapse.

### Cited Findings
**APRA AMCOS**
- [FACT] APRA AMCOS licenses the live performance, broadcast, communication, public playing or reproduction of its members' musical works and pays the fees to its 128,000+ songwriter, composer and publisher members. — [APRA AMCOS FAQs](https://www.apraamcos.com.au/about/help/faqs)
- [FACT] The **Online Mini licence** is for online services earning under **A$25,000 a year for each online activity**. It covers on-demand streaming, downloads, webcasting, podcasts, audiovisual streaming, **backing tracks**, and **karaoke** streaming and download services. It does **not** cover interactive streaming where users pick songs and build playlists, or semi-interactive streaming. — [APRA AMCOS Online Mini licence page](https://www.apraamcos.com.au/music-licences/select-a-licence/online-mini-licence); [Online Mini rate document (PDF)](https://assets.apraamcos.com.au/images/PDFs/Music-Licences/PEG_online-mini.pdf)
- [FACT] Online Mini fees: **Tier A A$275, Tier B A$550, Tier C A$1,100 per licence year for each category of music use, including GST.** Named categories include Karaoke Track Download (Tier A under 1,000 downloads a year, B under 2,500, C under 5,000), Backing Track Download (A under 1,500, B under 4,000, C under 10,000) and Karaoke Track Streaming (advertising- or subscription-funded, no downloads). — [Online Mini rate document (PDF)](https://assets.apraamcos.com.au/images/PDFs/Music-Licences/PEG_online-mini.pdf); [APRA Mini Online Licence Agreement portal](https://portal.apra.com.au/minilicence/Licence.action); [ABLIS listing](https://ablis.business.gov.au/service/ag/online-mini-music-licence/35018). *I could not open these to check the effective date of the rates, so treat the figures as current only once confirmed.*
- [FACT] Lyric reproduction and arrangements are not covered by AMCOS licences. Permission must come from the publisher, and APRA AMCOS will give out publisher contacts. — [APRA AMCOS FAQs](https://www.apraamcos.com.au/about/help/faqs); [Church guide](https://www.bbcatholic.org.au/ArticleDocuments/1566/Music-Copyright-Guide-for-Churches%20-%20APRA-AMCOS.pdf.aspx)

**Lyric providers**
- [FACT/secondary] The Musixmatch free "Developer" plan allows about 2,000 API calls a day and returns only **30% lyric previews**, for development and **non-commercial** use. Responses include a copyright notice and a **tracking script or pixel** that must be shown and run. Full and synced lyrics need a commercial licence. Commercial and enterprise pricing is "contact for pricing". — [FreeAPIHub: Musixmatch](https://freeapihub.com/apis/musixmatch); [PublicAPIs: Musixmatch](https://publicapis.io/musixmatch-api); [apis.io: Musixmatch plans](https://plans.apis.io/plans/musixmatch/musixmatch-plans-pricing/). *These are third-party summaries; developer.musixmatch.com could not be fetched.*
- [FACT] Reverse-engineered "free full lyrics" wrappers for Musixmatch exist on GitHub, e.g. [Strvm/musicxmatch-api](https://github.com/Strvm/musicxmatch-api). They are not licensed routes.
- [FACT] LyricFind is a Canadian licensed-lyrics provider serving 100+ platforms (Amazon, Google, YouTube, Deezer, Pandora and others). It licenses from 2,000+ publishers, including the majors. — [LyricFind (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LyricFind); [ProgrammableWeb](https://www.programmableweb.com/api/lyricfind)
- [WEAK, 2010] A small developer reported that LyricFind required a 12-month contract with minimum monthly payments, which was prohibitive for a small app. — [Hacker News thread (2010)](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=1862555). No current small-developer pricing found.

**How commercial apps license**
- [VENDOR] Yousician: its song catalogue is governed by licensing agreements with music publishers. Songs may be unavailable in some countries, and songs are removed when a licence expires. — [Yousician support: why can't I find a popular song](https://support.yousician.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000758937-Why-can-t-I-find-a-popular-song-in-Yousician)
- [FACT] Singa (Finnish karaoke streaming): a Warner Music Group master-recording licence (announced 4 June 2025) on top of about 120,000 "soundalike" re-recordings, a licence with Merlin for indie labels, publisher deals, and MassiveMusic for catalogue delivery and rights reporting. — [Music Business Worldwide: WMG–Singa](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/warner-music-strikes-licensing-deal-with-karaoke-startup-singa/); [Music Ally, June 2025](https://musically.com/2025/06/04/karaoke-firm-singa-strikes-game-changing-wmg-licensing-deal/); [MBW: Merlin–Singa](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/karaoke-startup-singa-secures-merlin-as-new-licensing-partner/); [MassiveMusic case study](https://massivemusic.com/cases/singa)
- [VENDOR/secondary] Simply Sing (Simply, formerly JoyTunes) is freemium. The licensed catalogue is behind a subscription of about US$89.99 a year, and a comparison site says the licensed catalogue "is the expensive part". — [Vocalify: Simply Sing alternatives (2026)](https://vocalify.me/guides/simply-sing-alternatives.html); [Simply (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simply_(software_company))
- [WEAK] Smule: I found only a Q&A answer saying its karaoke tracks are session re-recordings made under publishing and mechanical licences. — [Quora](https://www.quora.com/Where-do-apps-like-Smule-get-their-karaoke-music)

### Inferences
- **The Online Mini licence would not on its own make Note by Note's copyrighted songs legal.** It covers APRA and AMCOS rights in the musical works for an online service, but showing lyrics and adding synth arrangements need direct publisher permission. It is also an Australian licence and does nothing about the US-hosted public repo. A one-off call to APRA AMCOS media licensing (medialicensing@apra.com.au) would settle whether a free, non-monetised family PWA fits any category.
- Clearing even 10 popular songs would mean a deal with each song's publisher or publishers (often several per song) for lyric display, timed lyrics and arrangement. In practice publishers rarely answer hobby requests, and when they do they usually charge flat fees per song. That is inference; no fee data was found.
- The commercial apps confirm the model: their catalogues come from direct, paid, expiring licences and in-house re-recordings. None of that is available to a one-person free app.
- **Musixmatch or LyricFind in a no-server PWA:** the API key would sit in public client-side code, and the free tier gives only 30% of the lyrics, so it is useless for singing along. Its non-commercial and tracking-pixel terms would also add third-party tracking to a children's app. Not recommended.

### Gaps
- Exact current Online Mini terms (effective date, whether "karaoke streaming" includes on-screen lyrics, whether it needs revenue or allows a free service) could not be confirmed because the APRA AMCOS site was blocked. Ask APRA AMCOS.
- No published price for any LyricFind or Musixmatch commercial or synced-lyrics licence. No public details of Smule's or Simply Sing's publisher deals.

## 3. What is free to use: Australian and US public domain, traditional songs, traps, and Creative Commons sources

### Takeaway
A song is safe for a public site and repo when **both** the lyrics and the melody are out of copyright in **Australia and the US**. In Australia that means every author (lyricist, composer, translator) died before 1 January 1955 and the work was published in their lifetime. In the US it means the work was published in 1930 or earlier (as of 1 January 2026). Australia does not apply the "rule of the shorter term", so US public-domain status does not make a song free in Australia. All 8 songs currently in the app pass both tests. Watch for Kookaburra (in copyright in Australia until about the end of 2058), modern versions of traditional songs (Baby Shark, Mama Lisa translations), and US-only quirks (the Carl Fischer claim on Waltzing Matilda).

### Cited Findings
**Australian duration rules**
- [FACT] Copyright has expired in literary, dramatic, musical and artistic works published before 1 January 1955 whose creator died before then. — [Australian Copyright Council, Duration of Copyright fact sheet G023 (Arts Law copy)](https://www.artslaw.com.au/images/uploads/Duration%20of%20Copyright%20(G023v16).pdf); [ACC G023v19 (WA Government copy)](https://www.wa.gov.au/system/files/2022-04/ACC%20Duration%20of%20Copyright%20Sheet.pdf)
- [FACT] The 2005 change under the Australia–US Free Trade Agreement extended protection from 50 to 70 years only for material still in copyright on 1 January 2005. So authors who died in 1955 or later get life plus 70 years. — [Southern Cross University: length of copyright](https://www.scu.edu.au/about/copyright/key-copyright-concepts/length-of-copyright/); [Copyright expiration in Australia (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copyright_expiration_in_Australia)
- [FACT] Since **1 January 2019**, unpublished works by known authors get life plus 70 years (perpetual copyright for unpublished works ended). Works by unknown authors get 70 years from creation, or 70 years from being made public if that happens within 50 years. — [Mondaq: 2019 changes](https://www.mondaq.com/australia/copyright/772980/new-year-2019-sees-changes-to-the-duration-of-copyright-protection); [PROV: Copyright amendments](https://prov.vic.gov.au/about-us/our-blog/copyright-amendments); [Smartcopying](https://smartcopying.edu.au/guidelines/copyright-basics/how-long-does-copyright-last/)
- [FACT] **Australia does not apply the rule of the shorter term**, so a work's expiry in its home country has no effect on its status in Australia. Foreign works are treated as Australian. — [Copyright expiration in Australia (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copyright_expiration_in_Australia); [Rule of the shorter term (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rule_of_the_shorter_term)

**US status as of 2026**
- [FACT] On 1 January 2026, US works published in **1930** entered the public domain, along with sound recordings from **1925**. Compositions named include "I Got Rhythm", "Georgia on My Mind" and "Dream a Little Dream of Me". — [Duke Center for the Study of the Public Domain: Public Domain Day 2026](https://web.law.duke.edu/cspd/publicdomainday/2026/); [Internet Archive blog](https://blog.archive.org/public-domain-day-2026/); [beSpacific](https://www.bespacific.com/january-1-2026-is-public-domain-day-works-from-1930/)

**Traps and case studies**
- [FACT] **Kookaburra Sits in the Old Gum Tree** was written by Marion Sinclair in 1934. She died in 1988, and Larrikin Music bought the rights from her estate. — [Australian Dictionary of Biography: Marion Sinclair](https://adb.anu.edu.au/biography/sinclair-marion-15924); [Songfacts](https://www.songfacts.com/facts/marion-sinclair/kookaburra). Songfacts gives the expiry as "2038", and some sources say 2058. Applying life plus 70 to a 1988 death gives the end of **2058**, so Songfacts looks wrong (inference from the duration rules above).
- [FACT] **Happy Birthday**: the melody comes from "Good Morning to All" (1890s) by Mildred and Patty Hill. Patty Hill died 25 May 1946. A US court approved a settlement in 2016 that put the song in the public domain in the US. — [Smithsonian Magazine](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/the-forgotten-sisters-behind-happy-birthday-to-you-180983885/); [Patty Hill (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patty_Hill); [Brauneis, "Copyright and the World's Most Popular Song"](https://bpb-us-w2.wpmucdn.com/blogs.law.gwu.edu/dist/a/4/files/2018/12/BrauneisHappyBirthday-tcu5mh.pdf). I found no source stating its Australian status directly.
- [FACT] **Waltzing Matilda**: James Inglis bought the rights in early 1903 and had Marie Cowan arrange it. Her 1903 version is the best known. Carl Fischer Music registered a US copyright in 1941, presenting it as an original composition, and Richard Magoffin calls that registration invalid. The song is out of copyright everywhere except possibly the US. Banjo Paterson died in 1941 and Marie Cowan in 1919. — [Roger Clarke: Waltzing Matilda copyright](http://www.rogerclarke.com/WM/Copyright.html); [Waltzing Matilda (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waltzing_Matilda); [trishansoz.com](https://trishansoz.com/trishansoz/waltzing-matilda/waltzing-matilda-copyright.html). The sources conflict on when the US claim ends: Magoffin says 2011, and a 1941 work under current US rules would run to 2036. Unresolved.
- [FACT] **Baby Shark**: in August 2025 South Korea's Supreme Court ruled against US composer Jonathan Wright (Johnny Only). His 2011 version did not differ enough from the traditional campfire melody to be an original work, and Pinkfong's version differed from his. Pinkfong called the base song a public-domain "traditional singalong chant". — [CNN, 14 Aug 2025](https://www.cnn.com/2025/08/14/asia/baby-shark-copyright-court-pinkfong-intl-hnk); [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/news/entertainment/baby-shark-copyright-dismissed-1.7608715)
- [FACT] **Australian national anthem**: according to the PM&C summary, permission is not needed to use, perform, record or reproduce it for **non-commercial** purposes. Commercial use needs PM&C permission, and the words or tune may not be modified, parodied or demeaned. The same page says the Commonwealth holds copyright in the proclaimed words and in its own arrangements. — [PM&C: Anthem use and protocol](https://www.pmc.gov.au/honours-and-symbols/australian-national-symbols/australian-national-anthem/australian-national-anthem-use-and-protocol). *The summary's phrasing conflicts: "music and words in the public domain" against "Commonwealth holds copyright of the words". Check the page.*

**Creative Commons and free sheet-music sources**
- [FACT] **Mutopia Project**: 2,124 pieces free to download, modify, copy, distribute, perform and record. All are public domain or Creative Commons (the project's licence is CC BY-SA), in PDF, **MIDI** and LilyPond formats. — [Mutopia Project](https://www.mutopiaproject.org/); [Mutopia (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutopia_Project)
- [FACT] **Choral Public Domain Library (CPDL / ChoralWiki)**: mainly public-domain or freely available choral and vocal music, with PDF, **MIDI and MusicXML** downloads, "free to be downloaded and used for any purpose". — [CPDL (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choral_Public_Domain_Library)
- [FACT] **Mama Lisa's World** collects traditional children's songs from 100+ countries, but its content is copyright Lisa Yannucci, all rights reserved, including its English translations. — [Mama Lisa's World](https://www.mamalisa.com/)
- [FACT] MuseScore.com hosts many user-composed scores under **CC BY-NC**, and public-domain scores are marked as such. — [MuseScore forum: scores free and for sale](https://musescore.org/en/node/366450)
- [FACT] Free "children's music" stock sites (Chosic, Pixabay, Mixkit, free-stock-music.com) are mostly instrumental background tracks under CC BY or their own royalty-free licences, not sing-along songs with lyrics. — [Chosic CC music](https://www.chosic.com/free-music/creative-commons/); [free-stock-music.com](https://www.free-stock-music.com/children-s.html)

### Inferences
- **The current 8 songs** (from `js/songs.js`, read-only) all look safe in both Australia and the US:
  - Mary Had a Little Lamb (Hale 1830)
  - Twinkle (Taylor 1806, French melody 1761)
  - Ode to Joy (Beethoven 1824, sung on solfège, so no lyric copyright)
  - Are You Sleeping (traditional)
  - Row Row (traditional, 1852)
  - Happy Birthday (Hill sisters: Patty died in 1946 (sourced), Mildred in 1916 (from memory, not verified here), so expired in Australia under the pre-1955 rule; public domain in the US since the 2016 settlement)
  - Amazing Grace (Newton 1779, New Britain 1829)
  - Waltzing Matilda (Paterson died 1941, Cowan died 1919, published 1903, so public domain in Australia and also in the US as a pre-1931 publication; the 1941 Carl Fischer claim can cover at most new material in its own arrangement)

  The app's credit lines already show this provenance, which is good practice. One caution: the app's melody and lyric text should match the 1903 Cowan version, not a later published arrangement.
- **A two-test rule for adding songs:** (a) every lyricist, composer and translator died before 1 January 1955, and the work was published in their lifetime, which makes it public domain in Australia; and (b) the work was first published in 1930 or earlier, which makes it public domain in the US as of 2026. Songs that pass only (b), such as "Georgia on My Mind" (Hoagy Carmichael, who as far as I know died in 1981; not verified here), are still in copyright in Australia. Songs that pass only (a), such as an Australian song published in 1935 by someone who died in 1950, are still in copyright in the US for 95 years from publication.
- **Traditional songs are safe only in their traditional form.** Modern lyrics, verses, translations or recognisable arrangements (Pinkfong's Baby Shark, Wiggles versions, Mama Lisa's translations, modern "nursery rhyme channel" lyric variants) can carry their own copyright. Take the text from an old printed source or a public-domain archive, not from a modern video.
- **Australian candidates that look public domain** (each needs a per-song check): traditional bush and colonial songs such as "Click Go the Shears", "The Wild Colonial Boy" and "Botany Bay"; hymns and carols with pre-1955 authors (Hymnary and CPDL are good sources); and "Advance Australia Fair" with the official words unaltered (PM&C non-commercial permission). "Give Me a Home Among the Gumtrees" (1970s) and "Kookaburra" are **not** public domain.
- **CC licences:** CC BY needs attribution (title, author, source, licence link). CC BY-SA also needs any adapted version (the app's transposed note data) released under the same licence, which is fine in a public repo with a notice. CC BY-NC is compatible with a free, ad-free app, but it would block any future paid version. A per-song `licence` and `source` field in `songs.js` would make this easy to keep track of.

- I did not check Musopen's or IMSLP's licence terms. IMSLP applies Canadian public-domain rules, so check each item against both the Australian and US tests.

## 4. The "My songs" route: a parent adds a song on their own device and it is never published

### Takeaway
Typing in lyrics, tapping or singing a melody, or importing a purchased MIDI or MusicXML file onto a family phone is still technically a reproduction with no clear Australian exception. Fair dealing for "research or study" is the only arguable fit, and it is untested. **The realistic risk is very low**, because nothing is communicated to the public and nobody can see it. Enforcement targets public and commercial uses. The app's exposure is lowest if it never uploads, syncs, shares or exports user-added songs and its wording places responsibility on the parent.

### Cited Findings
- [FACT] Australia's private-use exceptions cover only format-shifting legitimate copies of specific formats (books and periodicals, photographs, film, sound recordings) and time-shifting. There is no general fair use. — [ALRC DP79, current law](https://www.alrc.gov.au/publication/copyright-and-the-digital-economy-dp-79/9-private-and-domestic-use/current-law-2/); [University of Melbourne: personal use](https://copyright.unimelb.edu.au/shared/using-copyright-material/personal-use)
- [FACT] Fair dealing for research or study has no fixed 10% limit; each use is judged on its own facts. — [libcopyright.org.au: exceptions](https://libcopyright.org.au/exceptions-to-copyright/); [Copyright Agency](https://www.copyright.com.au/about-copyright/exceptions/)
- [FACT] On MuseScore.com, "Official Scores" are licensed from print-music publishers (Alfred, EMI, Sony and others) and can be bought per score. Non-official, non-public-domain scores can be downloaded only by **MuseScore Pro** subscribers (about US$6.99 a month) and only if the uploader allows downloads. MuseScore says the download restriction exists because it must pay copyright owners. — [MuseScore forum: scores free and for sale](https://musescore.org/en/node/366450); [MuseScore (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MuseScore). *MuseScore's legal terms page (musescore.com/legal/terms) could not be fetched.*
- [FACT] GitHub's takedown process works on content in repositories. Content that exists only on a user's device is outside it. — [GitHub DMCA Takedown Policy](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/content-removal-policies/dmca-takedown-policy)

### Inferences
- **Legal position, in plain terms:** a parent typing their child's favourite pop song into a private, on-device song editor is in the same legal grey area as handwriting the lyrics on paper for the child to practise. It is technically a reproduction with no clear exception, but it is private, non-commercial, invisible and unenforceable in practice. The risk changes a lot once the song leaves the device: shared links, a "family song pack" posted online, a GitHub issue or commit, or a public export.
- **Developer (authorisation) risk:** under s 36(1A) a person can be liable for "authorising" others' infringement. The factors include power to prevent, the relationship, and reasonable steps taken. A local-only editor with no catalogue, no sharing and a clear notice is low risk. Including a lyric scraper, or a "search any song" feature that pulls lyrics from the web, would be much riskier. This comes from my understanding of the Act; I did not fetch case law (e.g. Roadshow v iiNet) this session.
- **Import from purchased files:** importing a MIDI or MusicXML file the parent bought or downloaded with a Pro subscription, for that family's own practice on their own device, matches what those purchases are for (personal practice). Whether a site's terms allow converting files into another app's format is unverified for MuseScore. Some sellers (e.g. Musicnotes, Sheet Music Direct) sell interactive or MIDI-capable files, and their terms were not checked. MuseScore's Official Scores licence lets MuseScore show the score. It almost certainly does not let Note by Note host it.
- **Technical fit:** MusicXML and MIDI parsing in plain JS with no build step is feasible (MusicXML is XML that `DOMParser` can read, and MIDI is a small binary format with tiny parsers available). Lyrics come attached to notes in MusicXML (`<lyric>`), which maps well to the app's one-token-per-note lyric format. MIDI usually has no lyrics, or has them only as karaoke meta-events (.kar).
- **Suggested wording:** *"My songs are saved only on this phone. They are never uploaded or shared, and they are not part of Note by Note. Only add songs for your own family's practice. Please don't post songs you've added anywhere public."* Avoid saying "legal" or "licensed". Do not include user-added lyrics in the "Copy results for Claude" report: song titles and scores are fine, lyrics are not.
- **Privacy:** local-only storage (IndexedDB or localStorage) keeps the no-server, no-account design. Note that iOS Safari can evict stored website data, and Home Screen web apps have separate storage from Safari, so offer an export to a file for backup. An exported file kept by the family is still private.
- **Age:** only parents should add songs. Consider putting the editor behind the existing parent-facing area or a simple "ask a grown-up" gate, so younger children don't paste lyrics from websites.

### Gaps
- MuseScore's current terms (personal use, import into other apps, minimum age) could not be fetched. Musicnotes and Sheet Music Direct terms were not checked.
- No Australian case or regulator guidance found on private, on-device transcription of songs for domestic practice. The "very low risk" view is inference.

## 5. Alternatives: original songs, commissions and collaborators; YouTube karaoke embeds alongside the pitch display; iOS microphone plus playback

### Takeaway
Original songs written by the children, with or without help from Claude, are the cleanest way to grow the library. Human-authored parts are owned by their authors (the kids), and anything with no human authorship gets no copyright, so it is free to use. Embedding licensed YouTube karaoke videos next to the pitch lane is legally low-risk because YouTube and the uploader carry the licensing. However, YouTube's terms forbid hiding the player or separating its audio, a children's site must self-designate as child-directed, and on iPhone the backing track will leak into the mic unless the child wears headphones. Since 10 December 2025, Australian under-16s cannot sign in to YouTube, so account-based parental controls do not work. Embeds still play logged out.

### Cited Findings
**Original and AI-assisted songs**
- [FACT] Australian copyright requires a human author and "independent intellectual effort". Wholly AI-generated output is not protected, while AI-assisted work can be protected where the final work reflects human creative input. — [Bell Senior: AI-written music and copyright in Australia](https://bellsenior.com.au/resources/technology/ai-music-copyright-apra-amcos-australia/); [Information & Communications Technology Law (2025), AI-generated works in Australia](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/13600869.2025.2486893); [University of Newcastle library: using generative AI](https://www.newcastle.edu.au/library/teaching-and-research-support/copyright/copyright-for-creators/creator-copyright/using-generative-ai)
- [FACT, recent; one law-firm source] ARIA's Charts Code of Practice was rewritten on **24 August 2026** to exclude wholly AI-generated recordings from the charts. — [Bell Senior](https://bellsenior.com.au/resources/technology/ai-music-copyright-apra-amcos-australia/); [Legal500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/australia/intellectual-property/supporting-human-artistry-wholly-ai-generated-recordings-disqualified-from-aria-charts-in-australia). *Not checked against ARIA directly.*

**YouTube embeds**
- [FACT] The YouTube API Services Terms and Developer Policies apply to all use of the embedded player. A **child-directed site or app must self-designate** with YouTube's tools, which turns off personalised ads and some player features. This applies even with Privacy-Enhanced Mode. Embedders must check a video's "made for kids" status before embedding and treat data from the player accordingly. — [YouTube Help: embed videos](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/171780?hl=en); [YouTube API Terms revision history](https://developers.google.com/youtube/terms/revision-history)
- [FACT] Privacy-Enhanced Mode uses `youtube-nocookie.com`. Any ads shown are non-personalised, and views are not used to personalise ads elsewhere. — [YouTube Help: embed videos](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/171780?hl=en)
- [FACT] Embedded players must be at least 200×200 px (480×270 recommended for 16:9). Developers must not "separate, isolate, or modify the audio or video components" of YouTube content, and must not hide, obscure or overlay the player or play in the background. — [YouTube API Services: Required Minimum Functionality](https://developers.google.com/youtube/terms/required-minimum-functionality); [YouTube embedded player parameters](https://developers.google.com/youtube/player_parameters)
- [FACT] **Recent (since 10 December 2025):** Australians under 16 cannot sign in to YouTube (supervised teen and pre-teen accounts included). They can still watch logged out, but account-based parental controls, channel blocking and content filters no longer work for them. — [YouTube Help: access restrictions for under-16s in Australia](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16758081?hl=en-GB); [eSafety: social media age restrictions](https://www.esafety.gov.au/young-people/social-media-age-restrictions); [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/world/australia/youtube-australia-teen-social-media-ban-rcna247124)

**iOS microphone and playback**
- [FACT/forum] On iOS Safari, turning on the microphone with getUserMedia changes the audio session. Playback volume changes (louder with echoCancellation on, slightly quieter with it off). Reported workarounds include setting `navigator.audioSession.type = 'play-and-record'` before opening the mic, or turning off echoCancellation, autoGainControl and noiseSuppression. — [Unity Discussions: iOS Safari volume changes when requesting mic](https://discussions.unity.com/t/audio-volume-is-changed-as-requesting-microphone-on-webgl-with-ios-safari/1656980); [WebKit bug 218012](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=218012); [WebKit bug 179411](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=179411)
- [FACT/blog] With Bluetooth or wired headsets that have a mic, iOS Safari can switch output to the speaker when the mic starts. Browser echo cancellation needs roughly 2 to 5 seconds to adapt. — [Medium: iOS Safari forces audio output to speakers with getUserMedia](https://medium.com/@python-javascript-php-html-css/ios-safari-forces-audio-output-to-speakers-when-using-getusermedia-2615196be6fe); [DEV: Web Audio echo fix](https://dev.to/hamedhajiloo/how-i-fixed-a-web-audio-echo-problem-with-a-5-second-delay-384h)

### Inferences
- **Original songs (best option):** short songs written with the kids around specific teaching goals (a song on just do-re-mi, a song with a single octave leap) fit a pedagogy app better than pop songs. The children own the human-written parts, so the owner should get the family's say-so before putting a child's song in the public repo, and credit them by first name only or a nickname to protect their privacy. Melodies and lyrics generated by Claude with little human input probably have no copyright and can be used freely. Check that they don't accidentally echo a famous song (search a distinctive lyric line, play the melody against well-known tunes).
- **Commissioning or collaborators:** a short written assignment of copyright, or a CC BY licence, from the songwriter is enough. A licence that is never written down is the usual way these arrangements go wrong.
- **YouTube karaoke mode:** a "sing with a karaoke video" screen that embeds an official or licensed karaoke video at 200×200 px or larger (visible) above the pitch lane is permitted. The app could not pull the YouTube audio into Web Audio, because the player is cross-origin and its terms forbid isolating audio. So there would be no synced "target notes" lane, only the child's live pitch line, plus timing from the IFrame API's `getCurrentTime()` if you author your own note map, which is a copy of the melody again.
  - Practical problems: the mic will pick up the backing track, including guide vocals on many karaoke channels, unless the child wears wired headphones. Bluetooth headsets may switch to call-quality audio or reroute output. Picking the right video means trusting channel licensing. YouTube end screens and related videos could show unsuitable content to logged-out kids, with no parental controls.
  - Privacy: YouTube sets identifiers and contacts Google even with nocookie, which breaks the app's "nothing leaves the phone" promise. Make it opt-in for parents, use `youtube-nocookie.com`, and self-designate as child-directed.
- **Simpler variant:** a "Song link" field per song that opens the karaoke video in the YouTube app or Safari. The child listens through headphones on one device and sings with Free sing open on another device, or on the same phone using picture-in-picture where it works. That avoids embed compliance work, but it is clunky.

### Gaps
- Whether `navigator.audioSession` is available in Home Screen web apps on current iOS (26.x) was not verified. There are reports of broken audio input in the iOS 26.1 beta ([Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/802555)), so test on the kids' phones.
- How licensed popular karaoke channels on YouTube actually are was not researched.
- Anthropic's terms on ownership of outputs were not checked this session.

## 6. What the owner should and should not do, ranked by legal risk

### Takeaway
Lowest risk: more public-domain songs (passing both the Australian and US tests), original family songs, and CC BY or CC BY-SA material with attribution. Next: an on-device-only "My songs" editor or importer, and opt-in YouTube karaoke embeds. Highest risk, and to be avoided: putting any copyrighted lyrics or melodies into the public repo or site, even "just for the family", and using unofficial lyric APIs or scrapers.

### Cited Findings
- [FACT] Rights-holders in Australian children's songs actively look for and pursue unlicensed uses (Larrikin with Kookaburra). — [Songfacts](https://www.songfacts.com/facts/marion-sinclair/kookaburra); [WIPO Lex: [2011] FCAFC 47](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/judgments/details/692)
- [FACT] GitHub acts on DMCA notices against repositories within about 1 business day and publishes them. — [GitHub DMCA policy](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/content-removal-policies/dmca-takedown-policy)
- [FACT] APRA AMCOS licences do not cover lyric reproduction or arrangements, so there is no single-licence fix. — [APRA AMCOS FAQs](https://www.apraamcos.com.au/about/help/faqs)

### Inferences
Ranked from lowest to highest legal risk:
1. **Add public-domain songs** that pass both the Australian and US tests, using old printed or public-domain archive texts, with a credit line. *Risk: very low.*
2. **Write original songs** with the children (Claude can help). Get the family's agreement, then add them to the repo, optionally under CC BY. *Risk: very low.*
3. **Use CC-licensed songs** (CC BY or BY-SA, or BY-NC while the app stays free and ad-free) with full attribution and a licence note. *Risk: low; check each song.*
4. **Australian anthem** with the official words unaltered, as non-commercial use. *Risk: low; confirm the PM&C page.*
5. **On-device "My songs" editor or importer** (lyrics plus tapped, sung or imported melody), with no upload, sync, share or public export, and with the wording in section 4. *Risk: low in practice, though technically a grey area for the parent.*
6. **Opt-in YouTube karaoke embed** (nocookie, child-directed designation, visible player, headphones). *Risk: low legally; the privacy cost and iOS friction are the real issues.*
7. **Licensing popular songs properly** (APRA AMCOS Online Mini plus per-publisher lyric and arrangement permissions, or a lyric API). *Risk: legal risk becomes low once cleared, but the cost and effort are out of proportion for a free family app.*
8. **Do not** put copyrighted songs (e.g. Kookaburra, Disney or pop songs) into `songs.js` or anywhere in the public repo or site, even "only for the kids", and do not rely on obscurity. *Risk: high relative to everything else; a takedown is the likely outcome.*
9. **Do not** use reverse-engineered lyric APIs, lyric scrapers, or extract audio from YouTube. *Risk: high (breaches terms as well as infringing copyright).*

### Gaps
- Whether a free, non-monetised family PWA fits any Online Mini category, and whether that would cover on-screen lyrics: ask APRA AMCOS.
- Whether the owner's plan allows a private repo with Pages, or another access-restricted host, which would reduce but not remove "communication to the public": unverified.

## Implications for Note by Note

### Takeaway
Grow the library through public-domain songs, original songs and CC songs in the public repo, and add an on-device-only "My songs" editor for the family's own copyrighted favourites.
