# Note by Note

Singing lessons with live pitch feedback, in your own key.

Open **https://lbecker17.github.io/note-by-note/** in Safari on your iPhone, then tap Share → **Add to Home Screen**.

## What's in it

- **Three tabs.** Today (the daily warm-up, your week and what's next), Lessons (Start here, Pitch, Breath skills) and Songs.
- **Range test.** Sing a low and a high note, or pick a typical range (Child, Higher voice, Lower voice). The app shows your comfy notes (for example A3 to D5), with no voice-type labels, and every lesson then moves into your key.
- **Live pitch lane.** Target notes scroll past a line. Your voice draws over them and each note lights up in its colour as you hit it.
- **Lessons.** Match a note, long holds, steps, scales, leaps, arpeggios, minor, an echo game with new tunes each time, sirens and staccato.
- **Daily warm-up.** About four minutes: stretch and breathe, small hum slides, a light "oo" pattern, a five-note scale, "today's challenge", a control move that changes by weekday (a steady hold, bouncy "ha", grow and shrink, or a slow slide down), then one big siren to finish. Nothing in it goes near the very top of your range, and the biggest slide comes last. Today shows the days you sang this week, aiming for three.
- **Warm up before songs.** Songs open once you've done today's warm-up, and stay open until midnight. Lessons are always open. The warm-up only counts if the app hears you sing every part, going up and down with the notes. It doesn't need to be in tune.
- **Songs.** Public-domain songs with lyrics and piano: learn them line by line, then sing them through. Line by line plays a phrase at a time, joining short lines up to a natural place to pause (the end of a sentence or clause, a rest or held note, a new verse), about 4 to 10 seconds each. Before starting, the child picks a speed: Slow (75%), Steady (90%) or Normal; each song remembers its last speed, and quick songs start at Steady.
- **Family songs.** A grown-up can add a song they've bought, from a karaoke MIDI file (.kar or .mid with lyrics) or MusicXML sheet music (.musicxml, .xml or .mxl). Before it's added you see the title, how it starts, how long it is and its lowest and highest notes, and you can play the tune, pick another melody track, and choose a part of a long song (a verse and chorus, say). Family songs open like the others: in your key, line by line then sung through, after the warm-up. Adding, moving and deleting them need the grown-up PIN.
- **Free sing.** Make up a tune (or pick an idea like "Sing like a sleepy dragon"), tap Done, and see it as coloured note blocks with a few kind words about it; play it back on piano or as a song with chords and drums, then sing again. It works from the pitch line only, so nothing is recorded.
- **Family account (optional).** A grown-up signs each family device in once, in Settings → For grown-ups → Family account, with their own email and password (each grown-up can have one; they all land in the same family). The app then opens on **Who's singing?**: each child has a singer card with a picture and an optional 4-number code, and their range, settings, scores, practice days and today's warm-up follow them to every family device. "Switch singer" is on the Today tab. Family songs added on one device show up on all of them. Under For grown-ups → Family: each singer's week at a glance, rename, change the picture or code, delete a singer, add one, and sign this device out. On the first sign-in, this device's own singing can become the first singer, and its family songs join the family.
- **Results.** Score, notes landed and how much of the time the mic heard you, with tips in plain words ("a little low", "a little high"). Singing the right note in another octave counts, and the lane says "same note, lower" or "same note, higher". Under "For grown-ups", a quiet "Copy results for Claude" link copies the details, in cents, for coaching.

## Good to know

- Nothing you sing is recorded or sent anywhere. Pitch detection runs on the phone.
- Without a family account, progress is stored on the device only. A run only counts toward your practice days and best scores if the mic heard you for at least a fifth of the singing time.
- Headphones let you hear the guide note while you sing. Without them, you hear each part first, then sing it back; in a song's Sing it through, the song plays straight through in its own time after one count-in, with a soft bass and chords that never hold the note you're meant to be singing (a line that starts after a rest of a beat or more gets its first note very softly in that rest). The warm-up always works that way, so sound from the phone's speaker can't count as singing. If Headphones is on but the mic hears the guide note itself (it sits exactly on pitch, a voice never does), the run isn't counted and the app asks whether the headphones are in.
- Works offline once it has loaded. With a family account, everything still works offline from this device's copy, and a quiet line says "Saved to family" or "Will sync when online"; it catches up when the device is back online. When two devices both changed a singer's scores, they're merged (best scores and practice days from both), not overwritten.
- **With a family account,** singers' names, scores, settings and the family songs (tune and words only, never the bought file) are stored in the family's private Supabase project in Sydney, readable only by the family's signed-in grown-ups. Singing audio is never recorded or uploaded, and "Copy results for Claude" still leaves song words out. Signing a device out makes it forget the singers and family songs (they stay in the account). There is no password email: a forgotten password is reset by whoever runs the family's Supabase project (Authentication → Users).
- **The grown-ups' switch.** Settings → For grown-ups → "Warm-up before songs" turns the song lock off or on. Turning it off asks the grown-up PIN (or, before a PIN is set, a quick times-table sum), so little ones can't do it by accident. It's a gentle nudge, not a real lock: the app checks that someone sang along, but it can't be certain. Reset progress and the How strict numbers (in cents) live there too. Safari and the Home Screen app keep separate data, so a warm-up done in one doesn't open songs in the other.
- **Family songs are private.** Only the melody and words are kept (in the browser's storage on the phone, never the file you bought). Without a family account Note by Note never uploads them; with one, they go only to the family's private account so the family's own devices can sing them. They are never part of this repository or the website. "Copy results for Claude" marks a family song as "(family song)" and leaves its words out. Private Browsing can't keep them: add songs in the Home Screen app.
- **Adding a family song.** Buy the song in Safari: at [midi.com.au](https://www.midi.com.au) (Hit Trax), one marked "Karaoke Lyrics" with a melody guide, or on [musescore.com](https://musescore.com) (with MuseScore PRO) a score with the words under the singer's notes, downloaded as MusicXML. It goes to Files, in Downloads (unzip a .zip by tapping it once). Then in Note by Note: Songs, Add a song, enter the PIN, Choose a file. Check it, play the tune, and tap Add song.
- **Move, don't copy** (without a family account). Song shops usually allow personal use only. To give a song to a brother's or sister's phone, a grown-up opens the song's "…" menu, taps Move to another family phone and sends the private .nbn file by AirDrop or Messages to the family's own phone. iPhone web apps can't open files directly, so on that phone save the file to Files, then use Add a song and choose it. Once it has arrived, the song is deleted from the first phone (a grown-up can choose to also keep a copy, if the shop allows it).
- **The grown-up PIN.** Four numbers, set the first time a grown-up needs one (after the same times-table sum), kept only as a salted hash. Once it's set, it is also what turns the warm-up switch off. Five wrong tries lock it for a while, longer each time, even after a reload. "Forgot PIN?" resets it after the sum, but that deletes the family songs, so it can't be used to get around anything. With a family account, it asks for the account's password instead, and nothing is deleted. The PIN belongs to the device, not the family.
- **Warm-ups get the voice ready and make singing feel easier.** No study shows they prevent voice injury. Not shouting, singing at a comfy volume, easy notes, and stopping when the throat feels scratchy or sore matter more.
- **Look after your voice** (from Today, any results sheet, the Songs lock, or Settings) has short voice-care tips for children and a section for grown-ups: what the app can't hear, how much singing is sensible, and when to see a GP.

Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript. No build step. The look is "warm paper": cream and terracotta, or warm cocoa in dark mode, with the Fraunces and Nunito fonts bundled in `fonts/` (SIL Open Font License, see `fonts/OFL.txt`).
Tests for the tune engine (`js/tune.js`), the family-song importers and the family account (the merge rules in `js/sync.js`, the Supabase client in `js/cloud.js` and the sync in `js/family.js`, against an in-memory mock of the family's project, `tests/mock-supabase.mjs`) run with `node --test tests/` (Node 22 or later, nothing to install).
